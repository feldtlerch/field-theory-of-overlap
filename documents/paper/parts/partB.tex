\section{The flow equation}
\label{sec:flow}

\subsection{The branch, chosen}
\label{subsec:flow:branch}
\secgrade{nec}%
Three theories fit the geometry upstream, and no argument selects among them. The
alignment cannot be a cosine of persistent objects, decay linearly, and live in a
noiseless world at once: any two of these exclude the third. At the poles the cosine of
persistent covectors is stationary in every metric, so a linear lapse of alignment is
unwritable there; an isotropic fade of caring decays the coupling and leaves the lean
untouched; only a diffusing caring \emph{direction} yields the linear law, and only in
the mean. The choice among the three consistent theories is empirical in principle and
open in fact; this document \textbf{chooses}, and posts the price.

\begin{statement}{mod:branch}{The branch statement}
  This document takes the \textbf{stochastic} branch: the alignment remains a cosine
  (\ftag*{op:cos}), lapse remains linear, and noiselessness is surrendered. The caring
  direction diffuses; the flow below is the \emph{mean} of that motion. The alternatives
  are priced in \zcref{tbl:branches}: the geometric branch trades the linear lapse for
  pole-vanishing decay; the scalar branch trades the cosine ontology for a primitive
  bounded lean. Sites that survive only under some branches are so marked, in the same
  ledger.
\end{statement}

\subsection{Confluence}
\label{subsec:flow:confluence}
\secgrade{cont-anch}%
The epistemic and ontic basins enter a single node.

\begin{definition}[\hdr{dyn:flow}]\grade{cont-anch}
\defines{\decayderived,\noisediff}\DisplayClass{dynamics}%
  On a maintained neighbourhood, the mean alignment obeys
  \begin{align}
    &\frac{d\,\mathbb E[\leanbare]}{dt} &=& \ \mathrlap{\willnet - \willdecay
        + \hingeA\,\normG{\surplus}\,\effdensity\,\bendfn(\mathbb E[\leanbare])
        \ \dimrate}\label{eq:dyn:flow}\\
    &\willnet   &:& \eqtag{w:net}{ontic forcing, the four signed wills (\ftag*{w:signed})}\\
    &\willdecay &:& \eqtag{dyn:lapse}{passive decay, $\willdecay=\decayrate\,\mathbb E[\leanbare]$}\\
    &\decayrate=\decayderived
      &:& \eqtag{dyn:gamma}{derived, not posted: wobble times room to wobble}\\
    &\text{bent formation term} &:&
      \eqtag{dyn:formclose}{\vtop{\hbox{the additive INTERIOR closure,}
                                  \hbox{\ $\hingeA\,\dimhingeA$, $\bendfn(0)=0$}}}
  \end{align}
  The flow is written in the alignment $\leanbare$, the operator's readable shadow; the stake
  rides beneath it, the held $\heldweight$ beneath that. The equation is the expectation
  under the sharp-hold closure, whose scope is stated at \see{subsec:fixed:exact},
  and approximate as a
  trajectory; the underlying motion lives on the sphere of caring directions, which
  preserves $\leanbare\in[-1,1]$ by construction --- the mean equation needs no boundary
  device, because it was never the dynamics, only their shadow. The closure has three edges,
  all posted: it fails at criticality, where fluctuations are large; it degrades at
  the poles, where any forcing that turns the caring direction enters the mean through a
  geometric factor that vanishes with $1-\leanbare^{2}$ --- the additive $\willnet$ is the
  interior form; and it flatters the founding regime, where a weak hold leaves the
  distribution near-uniform and the geometric factor averages to $(n-1)/n$, not $1$
  (\zcref{dyn:moment}) --- every unit of forcing buys less turn than the interior form
  books, exactly where founding happens. Only the decay term, the sphere Laplacian's
  degree-one eigenvalue (\zcref{dyn:gamma}), is pole-exact --- and the stationary
  structure downstream does not ride the closure at all: the exact map carries it
  (\zcref{dyn:exact}).
\end{definition}

Its dimensional closure needs the surplus as an \emph{anchored length}: $\surplus$ is
plural (\ftag{gen:obj}), carrying no scalar magnitude until $\anchormetric$ collapses it to
$\normG{\surplus}\ \dimwelfare$; only then does $\hingeA$'s inverse-welfare factor close
the formation term to a rate. That length is the edge's fact, not either agent's, and a
different $\anchormetric$ gives a different $\normG{\surplus}$ and so a different
$\alignrate$ --- so \ftag{dyn:flow} is \gradeword{cont-anch}: the heart runs on the public
commensuration and is metric-relative. The ontic basin supplies the drive and the bent
formation term; the epistemic basin supplies, at the next line, the verdict that the parameter
governing this equation is a rate, blind at a point.

One word must not bind two objects. The flow's drift $\hingeA\,\normG{\surplus}\,
\effdensity\,\bendfn(\leanbare)$ is the \textbf{formation term}: a rate, rising near
the origin, the field's own surplus-seeking. The reward of \ftag{f:rew} is a
\emph{potential}: dimensionless, peaking at the sealed stranger, falling as the edge
opens. Different dimensions, opposite gradients --- \ftag{gen:will}'s ``opposes the
reward gradient'' reads the potential, never the formation term. Binding both under
one word manufactures a dilemma at the moral baseline that the two-spend grading
dissolves (\see{subsec:good:spends}); the alphabet keeps them apart here, at the
source.

Every term is $\dimrate$ --- the equation is dimensionally closed, the act it describes
fittable. What is not fittable is the source of the forcing: pull and bind enter $\will$
identically, and the quantity that would distinguish them carries no dimension in
\ftag{gen:alph}.

\begin{definition}[\hdr{src:nd}]\grade{nec}
  The \emph{non-dimensional source} is the one thing the flow forces into its forcing term
  with no dimension to hold it. It is not a gap an instrument might later close, nor
  underdetermination over a known basis that convention might fix (\ftag{epist:duhem},
  \ftag{op:recess}), but a distinction the alphabet does not contain: the flow is closed in
  its arithmetic and open in its interpretation.
\end{definition}

\ftag{src:nd} is the seam the second half works and the wall the last section names.

  The confluence has the most structurally parallel machinery in all of economics sitting
  one assumption away: search-and-matching, Diamond--Mortensen--Pissarides \parencite{a:dmp}.
  A formation process, a separation rate that decays the stock, a steady state where the two
  balance --- \ftag{dyn:flow} is that equation, $\willdecay$ the separation hazard, \ftag{dyn:bif}

  reward gradient --- you search where the surplus is, $\will$ aligned with

  $\nabla\rewardfield$. Grant DMP its matching function and you have granted a rate that
  forms ties; the wall is that the rate it grants is the wrong sign for the only term that
  distinguishes weaving from extraction. The richest theory of relationship-as-rate the
  discipline has stops precisely at the will's type, which is where this document begins.
\end{econ}

\section{Fixed points: the trichotomy, and the seed}
\label{sec:fixed-points}

\subsection{The second gate}
\label{subsec:fixed:gate}
\secgrade{nec}%
With the will and the anti-will absent, the flow's fixed points are the gate-stride
\ftag{g:gate} at \zcref{tbl:sc:bif}.

\begin{proposition}[\hdr{dyn:bif}]\grade{cont-anch}\DisplayClass{dynamics}%
  The trivial $\leanbare=0$ is always a fixed point, and it destabilizes iff
  \begin{equation}
    \boxed{\;
    \underbrace{\hingeA\,\normG{\surplus}\,\effdensity\,\bendfn'(0)}_{\text{formation rate}}
    >\underbrace{\decayrate}_{\text{decay rate}}\;}\qquad\dimrate\ \text{balance.}
    \label{eq:dyn:bif}
  \end{equation}
  The will is absent from the threshold --- the boundary is set by formation and decay alone.
  One may assume naively that the same inequality governs the \emph{existence} of a held
  root $\alignfixed>0$; it does not, and cannot: existence is set by the global shape of
  the bend, and bistability requires the trivial root to stay stable. This
  inequality is one regime boundary of three. And on the
  stochastic branch its constant is corrected: the exact boundary compares the
  formation rate against the response constant $\respderived$, not the decay $\decayrate$
  (\ftag{dyn:response}) --- the closure's threshold is the many-goods limit, low by
  $n/(n-1)$.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{The three regimes}
\label{subsec:fixed:tri}
\secgrade{cont-anch}%
The bend's curvature carries a name of its own: \handle{dyn:bend}, sigmoidal
$\bendfn$, increasing returns at the bottom of closeness.

\begin{theorem}[\hdr{dyn:tri}]\grade{cont-anch}
\defines{\bendslope}\DisplayClass{dynamics}%
  Let $C=\hingeA\,\normG{\surplus}\,\effdensity\ \dimrate$ and
  $\psi(\leanbare)=\bendfn(\leanbare)/\leanbare$, unimodal for sigmoidal $\bendfn$ with
  slope $\bendslope$ ($\bendfn''>0$ near $0$). The interior structure
  is a trichotomy, not a threshold:
  \begin{align}
    &\text{subsidy} &:& \eqtag{dyn:sub}{$C\psi_{\max}<\decayrate$:
      openness lives only on continuous will}\\
    &\text{seed} &:& \eqtag{dyn:seed}{$C\bendfn'(0)<\decayrate<C\psi_{\max}$:
      bistable; one crossing suffices}\\
    &\text{spontaneous} &:& \eqtag{dyn:spont}{$C\bendfn'(0)>\decayrate$:
      the edge opens itself; closure must be funded}
  \end{align}
  In the seed regime the minimal founding act is dimensionless: $\int\will\,dt\
  \dimratio$, bounded below by the separatrix gap --- a finite \emph{turn}, not a
  quantity of welfare. The seed regime is where increasing returns at the bottom of
  closeness live: two basins, a sealed root and an open one, a separatrix between.
  The inequalities above are the closure's; the exact boundaries, and the factor by
  which each moves, are posted at \ftag{dyn:response} --- structure intact, every
  boundary up by at least $n/(n-1)$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{The exact map}
\label{subsec:fixed:exact}
\secgrade{cont-anch}%
The closure can be retired from everything stationary. On the stochastic branch the
sphere is not a metaphor but a computable world, and the fixed-point structure above is
that world's own, not the closure's.

Which fixes the scope of the additive flow, and it is stated here rather than assumed:
the additive mean equation is an interior first-moment closure. Near criticality, where
fluctuations are not negligible, it is not used as an exact trajectory law; stationary
existence and response claims are taken from the exact self-consistency map instead. The
first moment it approximates is exact and closure-free below (\ftag{dyn:moment}); the
stationary structure is the map's (\ftag{dyn:exact}), with the boundaries it moves posted
at \ftag{dyn:response}.

\begin{theorem}[\hdr{dyn:moment}]\grade{nec}\DisplayClass{dynamics}%
  (Scope: the stochastic branch, \ftag{mod:branch}.) The alignment is a degree-one
  harmonic of the diffusing caring direction, so for a constant forcing $\will$ the
  first moment obeys, exactly and with no closure,
  \begin{equation}
    \frac{d\,\mathbb E[\leanbare]}{dt}
      =\will\,\mathbb E\bigl[1-\leanbare^{2}\bigr]\;-\;\decayderived\,\mathbb E[\leanbare]
    \qquad\dimrate.
    \label{eq:dyn:moment}
  \end{equation}
  Three readings. The decay term is pole-exact: \zcref{dyn:gamma} is confirmed, not
  approximated. The forcing enters through the geometric factor, which vanishes at the
  poles with $1-\leanbare^{2}$ --- the posted degradation (\ftag{dyn:flow}), made
  precise. And under a \emph{weak} hold the distribution is near-uniform, where the
  factor averages to $(n-1)/n$, not $1$: the founding regime pays a toll the interior
  form never posted. The additive flow \zcref{eq:dyn:flow} is the interpolation between
  these, with irreducible relative error at least $1/n$ exactly where founding happens.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\hdrunbacked{The exact self-consistency map}{dyn:exact}]\grade{cont-anch}\DisplayClass{dynamics}%
  (Same scope.) At constant forcing the stationary law of the caring direction is von
  Mises--Fisher at concentration $\will/\noisediff$, and the mean lean is the Bessel
  ratio $\Abessel$ --- strictly increasing, $\Abessel(0)=0$, saturating at the pole.
  Harmonics of degree two and higher relax at least twice as fast as the mean, so the
  distribution tracks the shape set by the current mean --- an adiabatic grounding that
  holds in the weak regime where sharp-hold fails --- and the held roots of the flow
  solve one implicit equation,
  \begin{equation}
    \boxed{\ \alignfixed=\Abessel\!\Bigl(\frac{\will+C\,\bendfn(\alignfixed)}{\noisediff}\Bigr),\ }
    \qquad C=\hingeA\,\normG{\surplus}\,\effdensity\ \dimrate,
    \label{eq:dyn:exact}
  \end{equation}
  exact under the drift reading of the forcing, with no sharp-hold anywhere. The
  trichotomy \zcref{dyn:tri} is this map's root structure --- three regimes, bistable
  middle, separatrix --- intact.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\hdrunbacked{Two constants, not one}{dyn:response}]\grade{cont-anch}\DisplayClass{dynamics}%
\defines{\respderived}%
  The sphere hands the theory two dimensioned rates, and the closure spends one for
  both. Free means die at the decay constant $\decayderived$ (\zcref{dyn:gamma}); a
  weakly held mean answers forcing at the \textbf{response constant} $\respderived$ ---
  the uniform measure's coordinate variance is $1/n$, not the $1/(n-1)$ of the decay
  eigenvalue:
  \begin{equation}
    \bar\leanbare=\frac{\will}{\noisediff\,n}+O(\will^{3})
    \qquad\dimratio.
    \label{eq:dyn:response}
  \end{equation}
  Every regime boundary therefore compares the formation rate against $\respderived$, not
  $\decayrate$: subsidy $C\psi_{\max}\lesssim\respderived$; seed
  $C\bendfn'(0)<\respderived\lesssim C\psi_{\max}$; spontaneous
  $C\bendfn'(0)>\respderived$. The linear boundary rescales by exactly $n/(n-1)$; the
  seed regime's lower edge is a tangency of the Bessel map at finite lean, where its
  concavity bites, so the saddle-node moves by \emph{more} --- ``at least $n/(n-1)$'',
  never ``exactly''. In a three-goods reference world with unit diffusion and one
  representative bend, the closure's seed window $(6.7,\,40)$ becomes $(11.51,\,60)$:
  the lower edge by more than the factor, the upper exactly by it. Spontaneous opening
  is harder, the seed window higher, and the subsidy region --- where openness lives
  only on continuous will --- \emph{larger} than the closure posts. Every correction
  the exact treatment forces runs in the same direction: more of the social world is
  funded, less of it is free.
\end{theorem}

is a rate $\dimrate$ --- by \ftag{epi:fact}, blind at a point. $\surplus$ is discoverable

\ftag{g:knock} on $\biforce$ proves the latency.

And the latency is triply modal: blind at a point (\ftag{epi:fact}), anchor-relative

which is at criticality (\see{subsec:fixed:exact}). Two communities with different $\anchormetric$ disagree,
both correctly, whether one edge is latent-viable or dead; and where the question is
sharpest, the equation asked is least a trajectory. Contested development priorities

\subsection{Standing will against decay}
\label{subsec:maint:standing}
\secgrade{nec}%
Take any held $\alignfixed>0$ with the formation term absent and $\willanti=\willsever=0$.

\begin{theorem}[\hdr{J:occ}]\grade{nec}\DisplayClass{dynamics}%
  (Scope: the stochastic and scalar branches; priced at \zcref{tbl:branches}.) At
  $\alignrate=0$ the flow forces
  \begin{equation}
    \boxed{\ \willeff=\decayrate\alignfixed>0\quad\text{continuously, in expectation.}\ }
    \label{eq:J:occ}
  \end{equation}
  Fidelity is not a frozen state but standing will, a rate held against decay at
  $\alignrate=0$. The conditional is \gradeword{nec} and metric-free --- \emph{held
  implies occupied}, the flow at rest, no $\anchormetric$ in it. On the stochastic
  branch the boxed identity is the many-goods linearization of the exact occupancy map,
  \begin{equation}
    \will=\noisediff\,\Abessel^{-1}(\alignfixed)\ \ge\ \respderived\,\alignfixed,
    \label{eq:J:occ:exact}
  \end{equation}
  strictly increasing and divergent at the pole (\zcref{dyn:exact}): perfect fidelity
  would take infinite standing will, so every held edge lives strictly inside the pole
  --- and the posted rate understates the wage by at least $n/(n-1)$, double in a
  two-goods world. The conditional itself only strengthens: $\Abessel$ is monotone with
  $\Abessel(0)=0$, so held-iff-occupied needs no closure at all.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[\hdr{J:power}]\grade{cont-comm}
  One may assume naively that the standing rate is itself a dissipated power; a rate
  $\dimrate$ is not a power, and the alphabet can say so. The dissipation reading is
  priced, not free:
  \begin{equation}
    P_{\mathrm{maint}}=\willeff\;\edgeprice{i}{j},
    \qquad
    \edgeprice{i}{j}=\frac{\coupling{i}{j}}{\belongingradiusof{i}}\,
      \currency{i}{j}\,\privatewelfare{j}\ \ \dimnodewelfare{i},
    \label{eq:J:power}
  \end{equation}
  the marginal worth of the edge to its holder, spent at the decay rate,
  $P_{\mathrm{maint}}:[\,\nodewelfareunit{i}\,T\sp{-1}\,]$. The currency and the radius
  are posted; the weighting inside the coupling is held --- so the thermodynamic warrant
  for \ftag{gen:will} settles in arrears \emph{and} in a private currency
  (\ftag{snk:anch-t}). And the exact occupancy map raises
  $\willeff$ by at least $n/(n-1)$ (\zcref{eq:J:occ:exact}), the power with it: the
  invisible wage is larger than the linearized identity posts --- and two-part, once
  the coupling's own law is posted (\zcref{dyn:radial}).
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[\hdr{J:tremble}]\grade{feas}
  On the stochastic branch a held edge is distinguishable \emph{in distribution} from a
  frozen one: maintenance leaves a stationary wobble whose variance ties to $\noisediff$,
  and the measured pair --- decay of neglected edges, wobble of held ones --- must
  satisfy $\decayrate=\decayderived$ (\zcref{dyn:gamma}). The record can see that the
  glass trembles. What it still cannot see is the price: the held $\selfweight{i}$ by
  which the edge counts as a good at all.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[\hdrunbacked{The trembling curve}{cor:tremble:curve}]\grade{feas}\DisplayClass{dynamics}%
  On the stochastic branch the tie is sharper than a pair of estimates: the stationary
  law fixes the wobble from the mean alone,
  \begin{equation}
    \boxed{\ \operatorname{Var}(\leanbare)
      =\Abessel'\bigl(\Abessel^{-1}(\bar\leanbare)\bigr)\ }
    \qquad\dimratio,
    \label{eq:tremble:curve}
  \end{equation}
  one curve per $n$, \textbf{no free parameter}: neither $\noisediff$, $\decayrate$,
  nor $\will$ appears. A held tie's measured mean and measured wobble must land on the
  curve, or the branch is false --- the cross-check of \ftag{J:tremble} upgraded to a
  single-instrument, falsification-grade test, with no estimate of $\noisediff$
  anywhere in it. And what moves a pair \emph{off} the curve is exactly a rival
  mechanism --- a stock behind the rate, feedback in the hold, anisotropy in the noise
  --- which is what makes the curve an instrument and not only a check
  (\see{subsec:maint:stock}); each side of the curve carries a reading, completed at
  \zcref{dyn:ttax}.
\end{corollary}

The existential is not: that \emph{this} edge is held, $\alignfixed>0$ at all, inherits
\gradeword{cont-anch} from \ftag{dyn:tri} --- whether the non-trivial root exists is the latency
question, anchor-relative. Held implies occupied always; this-edge-held only within a shared
metric. It is the one thing economics has no result for, and the nearest miss must be
argued past, not around. One may assume naively that the joint also \emph{corroborates}
the type of \ftag{gen:will} from the will's own no-reservoir form; a consequence that
cannot fail carries no corroboration --- given $\decayrate>0$, no parameter draw makes
held-implies-occupied false, so the joint has zero evidential content about the posit.
It is a \textbf{transmission} theorem: it converts a measured decay and an observed hold
into the standing rate, adding no evidence of its own. The warrant it transmits is
collected upstream, at the data layer --- the measured decay itself
(\ftag{ev:decay-occ}), the release knee (\ftag{ev:knee-rate}), the identified residual
(\ftag{ev:resid-ex}, \ftag{ev:resid-opp}), under posted identification conditions (\ftag{maint:ident})
--- and is still settled at the price \ftag{J:power} posts: in arrears, in a private
currency.

\subsection{The stock rival, bounded}
\label{subsec:maint:stock}
\secgrade{feas}%
The strongest rival to the rate reading is not a rival ontology but a rival plumbing:
maintenance drawn from a \emph{reservoir} --- goodwill as capital, invested now and
drawn down in neglect. The framework does not refute the reservoir; it \textbf{bounds}
it, twice, with instruments this section already owns.

\begin{proposition}[\hdr{maint:knee}]\grade{feas}\DisplayClass{dynamics}%
  Let maintenance draw on a stock that drains with time-constant $\tau_K$ once
  investment stops. The released curves are exact:
  \begin{equation}
    \leanbare_{\mathrm{rate}}(t)=\alignfixed e^{-\decayrate t},\qquad
    \leanbare_{\mathrm{stock}}(t)=\alignfixed e^{-\decayrate t}
      +\bar\will\,\frac{e^{-t/\tau_K}-e^{-\decayrate t}}{\decayrate-1/\tau_K}.
    \label{eq:maint:knee}
  \end{equation}
  The rate world departs at slope $-\decayrate\alignfixed$ immediately; the stock world
  departs at slope exactly \emph{zero} --- the reservoir keeps paying while it drains,
  and the curve only bends into the decay as the stock empties: a \textbf{knee}. The
  deviation between the curves scales linearly in $\tau_K$, so a release experiment at
  measurement resolution $\eta_{\mathrm{res}}$ \emph{bounds} the reservoir: no knee at
  that resolution forces $\tau_K\lesssim\eta_{\mathrm{res}}/(\decayrate\alignfixed)$.
  The rate-type of \ftag{gen:will} is thereby the statement $\tau_K<\text{resolution}$
  --- a measured upper bound that every release experiment tightens, not a metaphysics
  --- and the reservoir is squeezed from a second, independent side by the stationary
  law: a stock smooths, so it trembles \emph{below} the curve of
  \zcref{cor:tremble:curve} at given mean, while a live hold trembles on it. Bounded,
  never excluded: the warrant is one-sided, and the framework says so
  (\ftag{ev:knee-rate}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{theorem}[\hdr{J:seal}]\grade{cont-anch}\DisplayClass{dynamics}%

  funded work (\ftag{e:closure}): the still center houses the toll-keeper on the same
  terms as the faithful.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[\hdr{J:seal-floor}]\grade{feas}\DisplayClass{dynamics}%

  spend of \zcref{eq:J:seal} vanishes. The sphere disagrees. In the spontaneous regime
  the origin is unstable at the margin's rate $\biforce$, and holding an unstable point
  still against noise is fluctuation-quenching work: for the linearized lean with

    \qquad\dimrate,
    \label{eq:seal:floor}
  \end{equation}
  a toll the first moment is structurally blind to, with a square-root cusp in the
  spontaneous margin --- steep just past the regime boundary. The still center's rent
  ledger was understated on both sides of the door: maintenance above the posted rate
  (\zcref{eq:J:occ:exact}), and closure at the origin above zero.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[\hdr{J:broker}]\grade{cont-anch}\DisplayClass{dynamics}%
  The true third party --- the tertius, the deterrer --- has no forcing term to spend
  (\ftag{w:torque}); what a broker can hold down is the edge's parameters. Because the
  seed regime is bistable, suppressing the witness density has two distinct targets at
  two distinct prices:
  \begin{equation}
    \delta_{\mathrm{crit}}
      =\frac{\respderived}{\hingeA\,\normG{\surplus}\,\bendfn'(0)},
    \qquad
    \delta_{\mathrm{sn}}
      =\frac{C^{*}}{\hingeA\,\normG{\surplus}},
    \label{eq:J:broker}
  \end{equation}
  with $C^{*}$ the saddle-node value of the exact map (\zcref{dyn:response}).
  \textbf{Deterrence} ($\effdensity<\delta_{\mathrm{crit}}$): the edge will not open by
  itself --- the trivial root is stabilized --- but it stays seedable, and one willing
  crossing defeats it (\zcref{dyn:seed} prices the crossing as a finite turn).
  \textbf{Foreclosure} ($\effdensity<\delta_{\mathrm{sn}}$): no held root exists at all
  --- the edge cannot be maintained even by a willing pair, and nothing short of
  restoring the density defeats it. Foreclosure runs several times deeper than
  deterrence in the reference geometry. Economics distinguishes entry deterrence from
  blockaded entry; the field derives that distinction as the gap between a linear
  threshold and a saddle-node, the price ratio computable from the bend.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[\hdr{J:forensics}]\grade{cont-anch}
  A sealed edge has two possible histories, and they leave different fingerprints the
  identification scheme reads (\zcref{maint:ident}). \textbf{Tail-sealed}: the residual
  returns a standing negative forcing while the anchored parameters read natural.
  \textbf{Broker-sealed}: the residual returns zero while the anchored observables show
  a \emph{shortfall} against their counterfactual. Residual, the tail did it;
  shortfall, a third party did. The caveats are the framework's own: the broker's
  fingerprint is a shortfall against a counterfactual, so broker-detection is available
  exactly where an anchor supplies the counterfactual density (\ftag{J:anchor} --- a
  natural desert mimics a suppressed oasis), and the test is one-sided, like every
  deep test this framework owns. The sorting rule that falls out is three-way: rent
  taken through parameters is landscape, never in the will register; rent taken
  through one's own forcing term is the funded seal (\ftag{ten:toll}); and a will
  spent against its own funding is spite (\ftag{ten:spite}) --- three addresses, each
  priced.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{The generator, split}

\subsection{The coupling's own law}
\label{subsec:osc:polar}
\secgrade{nec}%
Write the caring covector in polar form, coupling times direction,
$\caringcovector{i}{j}=\kappa\,\hat{\mathbf m}$, the coupling measured in units of the
posted scale (\ftag{c:kappa0}) so that $\kappa$ is dimensionless. The flow governs the
direction's shadow $\leanbare$; release evolves the full vector; lapse evolves the
direction alone. Three sectors, three implicit answers to one unasked question ---
what is $\dot\kappa$? A branch that owes a law of motion for the norm posts one.

\begin{definition}[\hdr{dyn:polar}]\grade{nec}\DisplayClass{dynamics}%
\defines{\atrophy,\ambientD,\willvec}%
  (Scope: the stochastic branch, \ftag{mod:branch}; the minimal linear completion ---
  linear damping, additive isotropic noise, the alternatives priced shapes.) Writing
  $\mathbf m$ for the caring covector, the posted law is
  \begin{equation}
    d\mathbf m
      =\bigl(-\atrophy\,\mathbf m+\genskew\,\mathbf m+\willvec\bigr)\,dt
      +\sqrt{2\ambientD}\;d\mathbf W,
    \label{eq:dyn:polar}
  \end{equation}
  with two new posted constants --- the \textbf{atrophy rate} $\atrophy>0$ (\ftag{c:mu})
  and the \textbf{ambient noise} $\ambientD>0$ (\ftag{c:Da}), both strictly positive and
  both rates $\dimrate$, which is what makes the floor below dimensionless --- the
  turning supplied by
  the branch's own skew sector \zcref{dyn:skew}, and one re-typing with consequences:
  the will enters as a \emph{vector} $\willvec$ whose polar shadows are two distinct
  spends. The tangential shadow turns the direction --- the flow's forcing, recovered
  as $\will=c_{\perp}/\kappa$, dimensions closing. The radial shadow $c_{\parallel}$
  \emph{deepens the coupling} --- a spend the norm-free theory had no symbol for,
  because a theory with no $\dot\kappa$ needed none. The sphere world of the heart is
  this model's frozen-coupling gauge: $\atrophy\to0$, $\ambientD\to0$ with
  $\noisediff=\ambientD/\kappa^{2}$ held fixed. The namesakes are classical --- the
  linear law is Ornstein--Uhlenbeck \parencite{a:ou}, the stationary direction von
  Mises--Fisher \parencite{a:vmf}; what is not classical is the transport: caring
  directions, atrophy as neglect, the floor as background acquaintance.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[\hdr{dyn:reconcile}]\grade{nec}\DisplayClass{dynamics}%
  Under neglect ($\willvec=0$) the one process reads two ways. Its first moment is
  exactly the release spiral,
  \begin{equation}
    \mathbb E[\mathbf m](t)=e^{-\atrophy t}\,e^{\genskew t}\,\mathbf m_{0},
    \label{eq:dyn:reconcile}
  \end{equation}
  amplitude dying at $\atrophy$, phase advancing by the skew --- the ghost orbit of
  \ftag{dyn:pitch} recovered verbatim, with $\operatorname{Re}\eigval=-\atrophy$
  setting the pitch's denominator. Its stationary distribution is exactly the lapse
  law: the direction goes uniform, the lean's marginal is
  $p(\leanbare)\propto(1-\leanbare^{2})^{(n-3)/2}$ --- a coin flip between friend and
  enemy at every $n$, mean zero and \emph{typical} lean far from zero. In two-goods
  worlds the law is arcsine, piling probability at $\leanbare\approx\pm1$: left
  alone, a two-good tie drifts toward maximal alliance or maximal enmity, with
  neutrality the least likely state --- neutrality is a many-goods phenomenon. One
  may assume naively that the sectors contradict, amplitude dying in release while
  the norm survives lapse; they are the mean and the distribution of one posted
  process, and the apparent inconsistency was a category slip.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\hdr{dyn:floor}]\grade{nec}\DisplayClass{dynamics}%
  Under neglect the squared coupling relaxes exponentially,
  $\mathbb E[\kappa^{2}](t)=\kappa_{0}^{2}e^{-2\atrophy t}
    +\ambientcoupling^{2}\bigl(1-e^{-2\atrophy t}\bigr)$, to the ambient level
  \begin{equation}
    \boxed{\ \ambientcoupling=\sqrt{\,n\ambientD/\atrophy\,}\ }
    \label{eq:dyn:floor}
  \end{equation}
  --- neither immortality nor death but \textbf{background acquaintance}, ambient
  noise held against atrophy. Three settlements at once. The accountability gate of
  the thirty-years-neglected tie stays open --- \emph{at ambient strength}, the level
  the remaining channel actually carries (\ftag{f:acc}). The \emph{true} seal is
  unreachable by any amount of neglect: given $\ambientD>0$ and $\atrophy>0$ ---
  the posted guards, and the boxed level is undefined without the second ---
  $\ambientcoupling>0$ wherever a goods space
  is shared, so sealing requires structural separation --- exactly the reading
  \ftag{f:sealed} posts (\zcref{f:seal-law}): neglect cannot seal; only structure
  can. And dormant-tie reactivation is neither miracle nor reductio but a decay law:
  the surviving mean is $e^{-\atrophy t}$ --- old friends pick up where they left off
  for $t\ll1/\atrophy$, at ambient acquaintance for $t\gg1/\atrophy$. Whether
  full-strength reactivation is a prediction or a reductio is answered by a
  time-constant.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\hdr{dyn:scramble}]\grade{nec}\DisplayClass{dynamics}%
  The completed branch makes the direction's diffusion coupling-dependent,
  \begin{equation}
    D_{\mathrm{dir}}(\kappa)=\ambientD/\kappa^{2}
    \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
    \gamma_{\sigma}(\kappa)=(n-1)\,\ambientD/\kappa^{2},
    \label{eq:dyn:scramble}
  \end{equation}
  so weak ties scramble fast and strong ties hold their aim: the single derived decay
  of \zcref{dyn:gamma} is the frozen-coupling shadow of a $\kappa$-dependent law. The
  phenomenology is exact and testable: a neglected tie first fades in strength with
  its lean stable, then, approaching the floor, the lean itself dissolves at
  accelerating speed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[\hdr{dyn:radial}]\grade{nec}\DisplayClass{dynamics}%

      & rotating ($j,m{-}j$ pair; Hopf at $\operatorname{Re}\eigval[j]=0$) \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
  \par\medskip
  Symmetrize the coupling, $\jaccouple\cyclicshift\to\tfrac\jaccouple2(\cyclicshift+\cyclicshift^{-1})$: the antisymmetric part is
  annihilated, every $\operatorname{Im}\eigval[j]\to0$, and the spectrum collapses to the real
  $\jacgrowth+\jaccouple\cos(2\pi j/m)$. The standing modes persist; the rotating one cannot exist. The
  prohibition is \gradeword{nec} --- the rotation frequency \emph{is} the antisymmetric part of
  the witness coupling, so with $\jacgrowth,\jaccouple$ real no anchored magnitude can restore an
  imaginary part a symmetric coupling has set to zero (the spectral theorem, read structurally).
\end{theorem}

The falsifiable content is a scoped prohibition: \emph{in the scalar, first-order,
instantaneous witness-coupling model, symmetric coupling cannot produce a rotating wave}
(\ftag{dyn:stand}) --- so a travelling phase-wave certifies the model is not in this class, by
exactly one of three exits: the coupling is directed, the witnessing carries a delay, or the
node-state is inertial. The positive claim is sharp once the other two are held shut: in the
first-order, instantaneous model a travelling phase-wave in $\leanbare$ --- open/close cycles
circulating a loop, period set by the imaginary part, no change in fundamentals --- is the
signature of directed coupling (\handle{dyn:rot}).

The synchronized flip is a single node's rhythm lifted across everyone; it needs neither
asymmetry nor coupling and is not what the prohibition forbids. The crossings are three, not
two: real eigenvalue through $0$ (the destabilization of \ftag{dyn:bif}), the symmetric standing
mode, and a complex pair crossing the imaginary axis (Hopf, rotating) --- the last alone is
the network oscillation, and it lives in $\mathrm{im}\,\readout$, recordable, orthogonal to
the kernel.

\begin{proposition}[\hdr{dyn:whereskew}]\grade{cont}
  Edge pitch and ring frequency measure \emph{distinct} skew sectors --- internal
  ($\genskew$, the curl of one tie's own dynamics) and relational (the directed part of
  the witness coupling, $\tfrac\jaccouple2(\cyclicshift-\cyclicshift^{-1})$). One may
  assume naively that the three instruments read one invariant at three scales; an edge
  can spiral in release while its ring stands, and a ring can rotate over edges that fade
  monotonically. Cross-scale agreement is a hypothesis of common origin; disagreement is
  not noise but \textbf{localization} --- it tells you \emph{where} the field's curl
  lives, in the ties themselves or in the witnessing between them.
\end{proposition}
