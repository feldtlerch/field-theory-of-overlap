\section{Goodness, the two faces, the baseline}
\label{sec:goodness}

\subsection{Goodness, located}
\label{subsec:good:located}
\secgrade{nec}%
Goodness is a will toward $\leanbare$: raising $\alignfactof{i}{k}$ where it was zero ---
setting $\assignweight{i}{k}>0$ where $\alignfactof{i}{k}=0$ --- a preference \emph{over} the
weights, identified with \ftag{gen:will}. Its object is the utility function itself, so no
fixed-$u$ representation contains it.

The two faces \ftag{g:split} by the argument each acts on: \ftag{w:exp} reaches for an unmeasured
$\surplus$; \ftag{w:vir} moves toward a someone. Both oppose $\nabla\rewardfield$, hence
coincide wherever $\surplus>0$; they separate as $\surplus\to0$, where surplus (extensive,
$\dimwelfare$) switches off while rent (intensive, $\dimratio$) persists --- so \ftag{w:vir}
stands alone at the \textbf{known modest good}: real benefit, no discovery, no witness. A
type-difference, not a posited crossover.

% generated: result:nrm:modest

``Modest'' names the epistemic and relational scope of the corner, not a threshold on the
magnitude of the benefit. Nothing here is a cutoff: a large gain read by its beneficiary's
own measure, needing no discovery and no witness, is in the corner, and a small one bought
with unread surplus is not.

The virtuous will itself splits: \ftag{w:enl} enlargement raises $\alignpair{i}{k}$ and is seen;
\ftag{w:res} restraint declines the right column and is not.

\subsection{The full baseline}
\label{subsec:good:baseline}
\secgrade{nec}%
\begin{definition}[\hdr{op:vmoral}]\grade{nec}
  The moral sign is a deviation from the \emph{funded trajectory}: the path written
  jointly by the field's own motion and the gradient of total privately-captured
  return --- both poles of \ftag{f:rew} in the gradient:
  \begin{granting}[the baseline converter is the ledger's one open debt: the
    registry posts its dimension, and what stays open is its calibration]{c:nu}
  \begin{align}
    \moralvel &= \alignrateactual-\alignrateself,
      \qquad
      \alignrateself=\fielddrift(\leanbare)+\framepull, && \dimrate,
      \label{eq:op:vmoral}\\
    \framepull &\proptoconst{c:nu} \nabla_{\leanbare}\left(
      \selfweight{i}\transpose\outcomechange{i}
      +\sum_j\foldchangeterm{i}{j}
      \right)
      && \dimnodewelfare{i}.
      \label{eq:op:vmoralself}
  \end{align}
  \end{granting}
  $\fielddrift(\leanbare)$ is the field's endogenous motion --- formation and decay,
  the flow with no will in it --- and the funded pull $\framepull$ is the gradient
  \emph{in the lean} $\leanbare$, in $i$'s own welfare units before the converter
  turns it into a rate. The baseline is
  a full counterfactual \textbf{trajectory}, not a forcing. One may assume naively
  that subtracting the funded pull alone would do; both candidates are rates and the
  dimension check passes --- but then the field's own motion leaks into the moral
  register, and a passive agent on a spontaneously-opening edge is minted virtue by
  the drift itself: a semantic type error no dimension checker can catch. Subtracting
  the trajectory removes it exactly:
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[\hdr{op:cancel}]\grade{nec}
  (Scope: the interior additive form of the flow \zcref{eq:dyn:flow}, under the
  both-drifts reading \ftag{mod:drifts}.)
  With the trajectory baseline the field's motion cancels identically,
  \begin{equation}
    \moralvel
      =\bigl(\fielddrift+\will\bigr)-\bigl(\fielddrift+\framepull\bigr)
      =\will-\framepull,
    \label{eq:vmoral:cancel}
  \end{equation}
  independent of the formation coefficient, the decay, and the lean. On this form the
  moral register therefore inherits \emph{none} of the dynamical sector's corrections:
  not the two constants (\ftag{dyn:response}), not the shifted boundaries, not the
  pole and founding factors, not the coupling's own law (\ftag{dyn:polar}) --- every
  exact correction flows along the dynamical axis and cancels out of $\moralvel$. What
  the disclaimer costs when the form is retired, and what survives it, is scoped
  immediately below.
\end{lemma}

The scope is not a formality, because this document retires that form from everything
stationary. The interior additive equation is a closure (\ftag{dyn:formclose}), stated
approximate as a trajectory at \see{subsec:fixed:exact}; on the exact branch forcing
does not enter additively at all but through the geometric factor
(\zcref{dyn:moment}), which vanishes at the poles and averages to $(n-1)/n$ under a
weak hold. So the subtraction has to say where the funded pull enters the motion, and
that is a modelling choice and not a theorem: the pull may occupy the same slot as the
will --- a drift on the caring direction --- or be subtracted as a rate after the
factor has acted. This document \textbf{chooses}, and posts the price.

\begin{statement}{mod:drifts}{The both-drifts reading}
  The funded pull enters the motion \textbf{where the will enters it}: $\will$ and
  $\framepull$ are both drifts on the caring direction, and the counterfactual funded
  trajectory is the same dynamics run at $\framepull$ in place of $\will$. The
  alternative is an \emph{additive-pull} reading, in which the pull is subtracted as a
  rate after the geometric factor has acted. Its price is a price on the \emph{sign},
  posted below --- which is why the reading is chosen here rather than left to
  whichever one a reader supplies.
\end{statement}

Under the posted reading the exact branch returns the lemma's cancellation and one
factor more:
\begin{equation}
  \moralvel=\bigl(\will-\framepull\bigr)\,
    \mathbb E\bigl[1-\leanbare^{2}\bigr]
  \qquad\dimrate.
  \label{eq:vmoral:cancel-exact}
\end{equation}
The two halves of the lemma part company here. The field's own motion still cancels
identically --- the half the definition's type-error argument needs, and it is robust
across the two forms. The disclaimer on magnitudes is not: the surviving factor is
exactly the pole degradation and the founding toll (\zcref{dyn:moment}) that the
lemma lists as not inherited, so magnitudes of moral spend are not comparable across
regimes without it. What does survive is the \emph{sign} and the \emph{zero-set}: the
factor is strictly positive off the poles, so $\moralvel=0$ exactly when
$\will=\framepull$, and $\moralvel$ carries the sign of $\will-\framepull$. The
partition of \zcref{tbl:good:spends} --- funded against unfunded, which is what the
normative half reads --- is the same partition on either form. That is the seam
between the paper's descriptive half and its normative half: watertight in sign,
scoped in magnitude, and this lemma is the weld.

The alternative's price, stated: under an additive-pull reading the residual is
$\will\,\mathbb E[1-\leanbare^{2}]-\framepull$, and where the factor is small ---
near the poles, and in the founding regime where it is at most $(n-1)/n$ --- its sign
parts from the sign of $\will-\framepull$. A will exceeding the funded pull can then
read as unfunded, which is a verdict and not a magnitude. Nothing above is an argument
that the additive-pull reading is wrong; it is the reason the choice is posted.

And none of the correction reaches the operational route, which never leaves
will-space: the identification scheme returns the will itself
(\zcref{maint:ident}), inverting the exact map rather than the additive one, so
comparing will against funded pull is a difference of two rates with no geometric
factor in it anywhere. The place the moral register is actually read is the place the
correction does not go.

The zero is normative, not computable, and it is doubly anchored: in \emph{metric}
($\anchormetric$, \gradeword{cont-anch}) and in \emph{internality}
($\selfsurplus$ unread, \gradeword{cont-comm}) --- two distinct dependencies, its error
a residual. The baselines coincide as $\surplus\to0$, the modest good again, where the reading
is cleanest.

\begin{econ}[label=e:baseline, title={Subtracting the right gradient}, ref=op:vmoral,
  cites={a:samuelson,a:internality}, departs=scalar,
  stopsat={prices the cost you do yourself across time; has no axis for surplus kept across the held edge}]
  Revealed preference \parencite{a:samuelson} reads static choice; the decisive datum is a
  velocity against the \emph{right} gradient. Subtracting only rent --- the naive selfish
  baseline --- over-certifies, scoring entrepreneurial surplus-capture as virtue-shaped
  wherever $\selfsurplus>0$; the correct baseline subtracts captured surplus too.
  Behavioral welfare economics has an ``internality'' \parencite{a:internality} --- a cost
  the present self imposes on the future self, \emph{within} one node's timeline --- and it is
  not this one: $\selfsurplus$ is captured surplus across the $\leanbare$-extended
  self, an interpersonal magnitude the single-node internality has no room for. Economics can
  price the cost you do yourself across time; it has no axis for the surplus you keep across
  the held edge, which is exactly the quantity the moral baseline must subtract.
\end{econ}

\subsection{The two spends}
\label{subsec:good:spends}
\secgrade{nec}%
Two deviations, one per axis, and every census below sorts by both. The
\emph{dynamical spend} is $\will\neq0$: forcing held against the field's own drift ---
what \ftag{J:occ} prices, what the kernel hides. Effort. The \emph{moral spend} is
$\moralvel=\will-\framepull\neq0$: deviation from the funded choice --- what
\ftag{op:vmoral} grades. Virtue or vice. The axes are independent
(\zcref{op:cancel}), and their crossing is a partition:

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering
  \captiontag{The two spends, crossed}{tbl:good:spends}
  \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{} l L L @{}}
    \toprule
    & \textbf{funded} ($\moralvel=0$) & \textbf{unfunded} ($\moralvel\neq0$) \\
    \midrule
    \textbf{working} ($\will\neq0$) & funded work: the entrepreneur, and the rational
      toll-keeper of \ftag*{J:seal} & virtue and vice proper: the modest good; the
      spiteful sealer \\
    \addlinespace
    \textbf{at rest} ($\will=0$) & lapse & the keeper's restraint: a moral spend at
      zero dynamical spend \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabularx}
\end{table}

Rent sits in the baseline --- the funded choice includes rent-following --- \emph{and}
$\willanti$ remains a will: holding an edge shut against the field's drift is work.
Two claims on two axes, no contradiction. One may assume naively that ``funded work''
is a collapse waiting to happen --- a chosen action subtracted as if it were gravity;
the baseline subtracts the \emph{counterfactual funded choice}, which is what a
baseline is for, while the actual gravity --- the field's drift --- cancels out of the
register entirely (\zcref{op:cancel}). Funded work is not a costume: it is
the exact name of the quadrant where the toll-keeper and the entrepreneur stand
together, effort the record cannot see and the moral ledger does not owe.

\subsection{The kernel takes its moral content}

Acts with $\outcomechange{k}=0$ against an unknown option give $\alignrateactual=0$ and
land in $\ker\readout$, separated only by the funded pull $\framepull$ (which
$\readout$ annihilates).

\noindent The first two are the occupied kernel --- \ftag{ten:res} concrete (restraint
against a paying option, no conjecture), \ftag{ten:fid} dense (every maintained edge,
standing $\will>0$ against decay, \ftag{J:occ}). The tenants are regions, not points,
and restraint's far corner is its purest case: a declined option-\emph{event} asks no
forcing at all, a moral spend at zero dynamical spend
(\zcref{tbl:good:spends}) --- nothing for any instrument to catch even in principle.
The third is the empty null: rest, no work, neither vice nor
virtue but \textbf{insufficiency}. The record sees $0=0$ across all three; the frame
that separates them is exactly what it cannot read. The full tenancy --- severance,
self-fidelity, the toll pair, the deontic --- waits on the descent
(\see{sec:still-center}).

\subsection{Two licenses}
\label{subsec:good:licenses}
\secgrade{nec}%
The same uncertifiability cuts both moral directions. \emph{Hope-virtue} (\ftag{w:exp}): a
blind, bounded-loss, stoppable experiment --- the unfunded self-breach would require
\emph{certainty} that benefit is impossible, which \ftag{dyn:bif} forbids establishing, so
unreadability is permission. \emph{Sight-virtue} (\ftag{w:vir}): reads a reachable cell
(self $\ge0$, others $+$), no superposition. The direct-legacy case is the lone exception,
where the discriminator is a standing state, present-tense (\see{sec:good-wise}).

\begin{econ}[label=e:commitment, title={Commitment, given a fixed address}, ref=op:vmoral,
  cites={a:degustibus,a:senrational,a:bowlesgintis}, departs=freeze,
  stopsat={carries the preference-revising preference as selection, never as a will}]
  \emph{De gustibus} \parencite{a:degustibus} forbids exactly this: a preference that revises
  preferences. \textcite{a:senrational} located the gap --- commitment, acting against one's
  own welfare ranking, escapes the single ordering revealed choice recovers and demands a
  meta-ranking --- but left it as critique; endogenous-preference economics
  \parencite{a:bowlesgintis} moves $w$ by selection, a desireless fixed point. The object is
  not unmodeled but unrepresentable in the fixed-utility frame: Sen's commitment given a fixed
  address, $\ker\readout$.
\end{econ}

\begin{econ}[label=e:license, title={The blind crossing, never the sighted}, ref=dyn:bif,
  cites={a:knight,a:weitzman}, departs=annex,
  stopsat={describes the blind crossing only; never the sighted good, nor unreadability as license}]
  Knightian uncertainty \parencite{a:knight} and optimal experimentation
  \parencite{a:weitzman} describe the blind crossing only --- never the sighted crossing at
  the known good, nor unreadability as a license.
\end{econ}

\section{Good versus wise}
\label{sec:good-wise}

\subsection{The temporal split}
\label{subsec:gw:temporal}
\secgrade{nec}%
Good and wise are both welfare states differing in time-index, not a state/rate pair:
\ftag{nrm:good} the present predicate, authored now, read plural; \ftag{nrm:wise} the forward,
settled later, singular. Helping a subject settles into \emph{enlargement} or \emph{martyr};
harming settles into \emph{exploitation-that-paid} or \emph{spite-that-backfired} --- and
the row cannot be read at the instant. The act is half-chosen (the column is yours) and
half-fated (the row is the network's settling).

\subsection{Self-funding uniqueness}
\label{subsec:gw:uniqueness}
\secgrade{feas}%
\begin{theorem}[\hdr{nrm:self}]\grade{feas}
  The wise region is the non-negative orthant on the extended self, and the boundary
  rule rests on three axioms, separable, each refusable on its own: \emph{own-measure
  booking} --- others enter only through their welfare by their own measure, the
  anti-annexation clause (\ftag{J:hip}); \emph{non-depletion} --- a pure transfer, an
  act with no co-movement and zero lived return, never raises the extended self; and
  \emph{no over-booking} \handle{nrm:maxbook} --- on the own-measure ray, booked
  content never exceeds the
  positive part of the lived return
  \begin{align}
    &\livedreturn{i}{k} := \livedreturndef{i}{k}
      =\selfweight{i}\transpose\comov{i}{k}\outcomechange{k}
      \quad \dimnodewelfare{i}
      &:& \eqtag*{eq:normative:lived-return}{\vtop{\hbox{(the felt total \ftag*{f:felt},}
                                   \hbox{\ at the boundary)}}}
  \end{align}
  --- currency-free and $\anchormetric$-free. The theorem then splits in two. The
  \emph{support} is forced by non-depletion alone: who can be inside at all is exactly
  the positively-aligned held edges --- any $w$-keyed rule admits unfunded depletion,
  any rule wider than held overlap re-imports it. The \emph{magnitude} is forced by
  no-over-booking up to the ledger: among admissible rules there is a unique
  \textbf{maximal} one --- it books exactly the lived return on the own-measure ray,
  any larger rule over-books, any smaller leaves funded content unbooked --- and
  $b=\holding{i}{k}$ is that rule in the \emph{funding gauge}, the posted currency
  equal to the belonging radius per unit of the other's anchored length. The shape is
  forced; the scale is the ledger's, already posted (\ftag{P:leak}). Love as large as
  honesty allows, and no larger.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[\hdr{nrm:nodepl}]\grade{nec}
  Non-depletion itself is a posted axiom, provably independent of the field: a
  prioritarian rule --- book distant others by a posted need-weight, decreasing in
  their welfare level, regardless of co-movement --- is dimensionally legal on the
  same field and violates non-depletion \emph{by design}, since on that ethics a
  transfer to the distant worst-off should count. No rule satisfies both
  non-depletion and need-responsiveness on strangers; the disagreement is
  axiom-level, and neither side can theorem it away. What the framework says back is
  one thing only: \emph{post your converter}. The rival must put a new object on its
  ledger --- the need-shape --- where the Stoic drawing spends only converters the
  descriptive apparatus already posts. The honest advantage is parsimony of posted
  converters, not truth: the choice between the ledgers is a value-theory made
  visible (\ftag{P:leak}), priced, never refuted.
\end{proposition}

\begin{insight}[\hdr{nrm:funding}]\grade{nec}
  The only necessary content is the type of the funding fact. The public line is anchored;
  the tipping point is anchored twice.
\end{insight}

\begin{remark}[\hdr{nrm:martyr}]\grade{cont-anch}
  The self-floor is private in funding type and public only at its threshold. A martyrdom
  line therefore has two facets: the lived return $\livedreturn{i}{k}\in\dimnodewelfare{i}$
  and the anchored public support that locates where the gift tips.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[\hdrunbacked{Triage currency cancellation}{J:triage}]\grade{nec}
  The worth-comparison component of triage cancels the chooser's welfare unit:
  \begin{equation}
    \frac{\currency{i}{k}}{\currency{i}{m}}
    :
    \frac{\dimnodewelfareratio{i}{k}}{\dimnodewelfareratio{i}{m}}
    =\dimnodewelfareratio{m}{k}.
    \label{eq:J:triage-cancel}
  \end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\refrain{}{When triage reaches for worth, it finds no height of self --- only a posted rate between the others, which someone hoped to read as fate.}

So virtue is \textbf{enlargement} --- build $\holding{i}{k}>0$ until $k$ enters a self that still
holds the lived self, self-funding through positive holding --- and \textbf{not transfer} --- holding
$u_k$ up by driving lived $u_i$ down across $\holding{i}{k}=0$, unfunded. The
choice-forced content reduces to a single desideratum, self-funding; the Stoic drawing of the
boundary through held edges is the forced rule under non-depletion, not a taste.

And because inclusion folds each member by \emph{their} weighting (\ftag{nrm:incl}, the aligned
stake, not the cosine and not $\selfweight{i}$), widening the self is never annexing the other
--- self-funding carries the intensity of the holding, so spending heavily on a faint tie is
not made costless by a high lean. The extended self is therefore \gradeword{cont-comm}: privately
weighted, dark, settled by the holder's own $\selfweight{i}$.

\subsection{The bottom-left, resolved}
\label{subsec:gw:bottom-left}
\secgrade{nec}%
Sacrifice at lived-self $-$, others $+$ is three positions of one self-boundary, recovered by
\ftag{g:rec}:

The grenade for a child ($\holding{i}{k}>0$ and large) is (self $+$, others $+$), wise, its

($\holding{i}{k}=0$) is (self $-$, others $+$), good not wise --- goodness
exceeding wisdom, the proof that the two predicates are not one. Sacrifice for a stranger is
wise only to the degree holding is already positive: widen until they are not a stranger ---
funded only to the radius posted at \ftag{nrm:incl}, and the act is funded.

\begin{econ}[label=e:extself, title={The household at fixed weights}, ref=nrm:self,
  cites={a:beckerhh}, departs=freeze,
  stopsat={aggregates members at fixed altruism weights, not at held overlap the will can widen}]
  The nearest economic extended self is \textcite{a:beckerhh}'s household and its rotten-kid
  theorem --- but it aggregates members at fixed altruism weights $w$, not at held overlap
  $\leanbare$ the agent can widen by will. No economic axis carries an extended self drawn along
  held edges; the uniqueness theorem makes the Stoic drawing the forced one under
  non-depletion, converting a philosophical choice into a structural property.
\end{econ}

\begin{statement}{}{Love and wisdom}
The holding that draws the self-boundary along held edges is \virtue{love} ---
\ftag{nrm:self} under the uniqueness theorem; it funds the grenade where mere weight cannot,
and love overshooting held edges is goodness-without-wisdom, not vice. The guard of the whole
orthant boundary, refusing self-erasure on one wall and exploitation on the other, is
\virtue{wisdom} --- \ftag{J:hip}.
\end{statement}
