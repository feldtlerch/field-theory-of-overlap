\subsection{Alphabet and fact}
\label{subsec:epi:fact}
\secgrade{nec}%
Two readable dimensions, one not: $\dimwelfare$ an outcome length after a converter,
$\dimratio$ a ratio computable against an anchor, $\dimrate$ a flow with no value at an
instant. The fact is the claim that point-readability tracks dimension --- scoped, per
\ftag{epi:shadow}, to the single-instant instrument.

\begin{postulate}[\hdr{epi:fact}]\grade{nec}
  A single state-snapshot cannot decompose a zero net rate $\alignrate=0$ into its opposing
  forces; $\dimrate$-at-rest is unrecoverable from one instant.
  \begin{equation}
    \boxed{\;\alignrate=0\ \text{(net)}\ \;\not\Rightarrow\;\
    \text{the forcing }\will\ \text{and the decay }\willdecay\ \text{separately.}\;}
    \label{eq:epi:decompose}
  \end{equation}
\end{postulate}

This is the operational form of \ftag{epi:fact}, and its honest scope is the instrument
named in it: \emph{one snapshot}. A time series reads persistence; the fluctuation record
reads maintenance (\ftag{J:tremble}); a probe reads the restoring force. What survives
every enrichment of the instrument is the converter principle's residue
(\ftag{epi:conv}): the price, spent into no record.

\subsection{Scope}
\label{subsec:epi:scope}
\secgrade{nec}%
Let $\readout$ be the readout, the real-linear map carrying motion to record. Then
\ftag{g:proj} splits any moral velocity $\moralvel\ \dimrate$ into a recoverable part and a permanent
remainder:%
\handle*{op:vel}\handle*{op:null}
\begin{align}
  \moralvelocity  &=\Pi_{\mathrm{im}\,\readout}\,\moralvel && (\text{state moved, recoverable}),
    \label{eq:epi:vel}\\
  \moralnull &=\Pi_{\ker\readout}\,\moralvel          && (\text{no motion}).
    \label{eq:epi:null}
\end{align}
Scope is image; out of scope is the permanent kernel, $\dimrate$-at-rest --- the still
center the table isolated.

\subsection{The moral grid}
\label{subsec:epi:grid}
\secgrade{nec}%
\handle*{nrm:good}\handle*{nrm:wise}%
Two independent questions attach to any act: its effect on a subject, and its effect on the
actor's extended self --- each an outcome read in a welfare unit.

\begin{definition}[\hdr{nrm:grid}]\grade{nec}
  The grid is $\dimnodewelfare{k}\times\dimnodewelfare{i}$, and \emph{good} and \emph{wise} are its two
  readings, differing not in dimension but in time-index:
  \begin{equation}
    \boxed{\underbrace{\text{good}}_{\dimnodewelfare{k,\text{now}}}\Big/
           \underbrace{\text{wise}}_{\dimnodewelfare{i,\text{fwd}}}=\text{spot}/\text{forward}
    \quad\neq\quad
    \underbrace{\text{state}}_{\dimwelfare}\Big/\underbrace{\text{rate}}_{\dimrate}
    =\text{grid}/\text{will}.}
    \label{eq:nrm:grid}
  \end{equation}
  \emph{Good} is the present predicate, \emph{wise} the forward; the will that moves through
  the grid is a rate, outside it.
\end{definition}

The \ftag{g:knock} forbids two collapses at once: \emph{good} $=$ \emph{wise} (one welfare
time-index read as another) and \emph{good} $=$ \emph{virtue} ($\dimwelfare$ read as
$\dimrate$).

\subsection{The operator, and its readings}
\label{subsec:ont:operator}
\secgrade{cont-anch}%
\handle*{c:Gstar}%
$\comov{i}{j}$ is directed ($\comov{i}{j}\neq\comov{j}{i}$) and carries the full cross-good
structure. Two covectors live in one dual space $\dualspace$: the \emph{caring}
covector $\caringcovector{i}{j}=\caringdef{i}{j}$, how $i$'s felt welfare moves per
unit of each $j$-good, and the \emph{feeling} covector $\feelingcovector{j}$, how $j$ values
the same goods. Alignment is projective and currency-free; inclusion is additive and needs
currency. One may assume naively that the operator has two readings, a direction and a
magnitude; the typed grammar finds four, and their spend-profiles differ --- that
difference is the section's content.

\begin{definition}[\hdrtitleonly{Alignment, standing and event}]\grade{nec}
\defines{\alignstand,\alignevent}
  The standing alignment reads caring against the other's \emph{values}; the event
  alignment reads caring against what \emph{happened}; the coupling is the caring-norm
  the standing cosine divides out:
  \begin{align}
    \alignstand{i}{j} &= \frac{\ipGcov{\caringcovector{i}{j}}{\feelingcovector{j}}}
      {\normGcov{\caringcovector{i}{j}}\,\normGcov{\feelingcovector{j}}}
      &:& \eqtag{op:cos}{standing: the held lean of two fates}\\
    \alignevent{i}{j} &= \frac{\pairing{\caringcovector{i}{j}}{\outcomechange{j}}}
      {\normGcov{\caringcovector{i}{j}}\,\normGvec{\outcomechange{j}}}
      &:& \eqtag{op:cosev}{event: the lean of one act}\\
    \coupling{i}{j} &= \normGcov{\caringcovector{i}{j}}\ \dimnodeperpublic{i}
      &:& \eqtag{f:couple}{coupling, the caring-norm}
  \end{align}
  Both cosines are $\dimratio$, defined where their denominators are nonzero, and they
  need not agree: a well-leaning friend can land an ill-leaning act. Normalization to
  $\dimratio$ is the erasure of magnitude: the cosines carry pure direction, the coupling
  $\coupling{i}{j}$ is exactly what they divide out.
\end{definition}

The coupling is bounded, not free:
$\coupling{i}{j}\le\normGcov{\selfweight{i}}\,\opnormG{\comovfield}$ --- projecting onto your
own welfare line can only lose coupling, and the gap is the entanglement your weighting
hides.

\begin{definition}[\hdr{f:felt}]\grade{nec}
\defines{\felttotal,\feltstake,\feltsign}
  What an event costs or pays $i$, by $i$'s own caring, is the metric-free pairing ---
  covector against vector, \textbf{no converter spent}:
  \begin{align}
    \felttotal{i}{j} &= \pairing{\caringcovector{i}{j}}{\outcomechange{j}}
      \quad \dimnodewelfare{i}
      &:& \eqtag{eq:f:felt}{the felt total, invariant}\\
    \feltstake{i}{j} & &:& \eqtag{f:feltmag}{its modulus, the felt stake}\\
    \feltsign{i}{j}  & &:& \eqtag{f:feltsign}{its sign: helped or hurt, anchor-free}
  \end{align}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[\hdr{f:split}]\grade{cont-anch}
  The felt total exists without the anchor; its \emph{decomposition} does not:
  \begin{equation}
    \felttotal{i}{j}
      \;=\;\underbrace{\alignevent{i}{j}}_{\text{lean}}\;
           \underbrace{\coupling{i}{j}}_{\text{coupling}}\;
           \underbrace{\normGvec{\outcomechange{j}}}_{\text{anchored size}},
    \label{eq:f:split}
  \end{equation}
  where each right-hand factor spends \ftag{c:Gstar} and the left side spends nothing.
  What you felt is \gradeword{nec}; how much of it was lean and how much was size is
  \gradeword{cont-anch}. Two communities disagreeing about an event's size and lean can
  still agree what it cost its victim --- and must, \emph{once the victim's weighting is
  supplied}: the total is built on no anchor, but it is read through the held
  $\selfweight{i}$ (\ftag{mod:facets}), so the agreement is over the invariant, not an
  access either community owns. And the access itself is dated: the felt total is
  collected by a biography, in arrears --- at the instant it is felt, not yet summed;
  nothing reads it now (\ftag{epi:fact}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{definition}[\hdrunbacked{Capacity and realized stake}{f:cap}]\grade{nec}
\defines{\capgate,\realizedstake}
  \begin{align}
    \capgate{i}{j} & &:& \eqtag{eq:f:cap}{the gate: what \emph{could} move, $\dimratio$}\\
    \realizedstake{i}{j} & &:& \eqtag{f:stake}{the charge: what \emph{did} move, $\dimwelfare$}
  \end{align}
  $\capgate{i}{j}=0\iff\comov{i}{j}=0$, in every metric: only the genuine stranger seals.
  The realized stake vanishes on $\ker\comov{i}{j}$ --- an act may miss today over a live
  coupling. Gate on capacity; charge on realization; never swap them.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[\hdr{f:ceiling}]\grade{cont-anch}
  By Cauchy--Schwarz, on any edge and any event,
  \begin{equation}
    \feltstake{i}{j}\;\le\;\coupling{i}{j}\,\normGvec{\outcomechange{j}},
    \label{eq:f:ceiling}
  \end{equation}
  with equality iff $\outcomechange{j}$ is aimed along the caring direction. The coupling
  is the edge's \textbf{price ceiling}: the most one anchored unit of event can cost its
  holder --- and the worst case is the targeted one.
\end{proposition}

\begin{statement}{mod:facets}{Construction and access}
  Every object above carries two gradings, not one: what it is \textbf{built on} (the
  spend set: the compiler's column) and what it is \textbf{read through} (the access
  route: the reader's column). The felt total (\ftag*{f:felt}) is built on nothing and
  read only through a held $\selfweight{i}$; the realized stake (\ftag*{f:stake}) is
  built on the anchor and read by anyone who shares it. Neither column collapses into
  the other.
\end{statement}

\subsection{The loom: two nodes, two goods}
\label{subsec:ont:loom}
\secgrade{cont-anch}%
Every object above, computed once, in the smallest world that carries them:
the loom. Two nodes, Alice and Bob; two shared goods, bread and song. The toy
posts its converters up front --- it cannot be built otherwise, which is the pedagogy ---
and holds one, which it shows only because a toy is omniscient:
\begin{center}
  \begin{tabular}{ll}
    \toprule
    posted & $\anchormetric=I$;\quad $\belongingradiusof{A}=1$ \\
    held (shown here only) & $\selfweight{A}=(1,2)$,\quad $\selfweight{B}=(3,1)$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{center}
Let the edge carry $\comov{A}{B}=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$:
Bob's bread feeds Alice; Bob's song drowns hers out. Then
\begin{equation}
  \caringcovector{A}{B}=\selfweight{A}\transpose\comov{A}{B}=(1,-2),\qquad
  \alignstand{A}{B}=\frac{(1)(3)+(-2)(1)}{\sqrt5\,\sqrt{10}}
    =\frac{1}{\sqrt{50}}\approx+0.14,
  \label{eq:loom:standing}
\end{equation}
mild friends by standing. Now let one event land, $\outcomechange{B}=(1,1)$ --- more
bread, more song, unambiguously good for Bob ($\selfweight{B}\transpose\outcomechange{B}=4$):
\begin{equation}
  \felttotal{A}{B}=\pairing{\caringcovector{A}{B}}{\outcomechange{B}}=1-2=-1,\qquad
  \alignevent{A}{B}=\frac{-1}{\sqrt5\,\sqrt2}\approx-0.32.
  \label{eq:loom:event}
\end{equation}
The standing and event cosines split in a $2\times2$: a well-leaning friend, hurt by
what helps him. The ceiling (\ftag{f:ceiling}) checks:
$\feltstake{A}{B}=1\le\coupling{A}{B}\,\normGvec{\outcomechange{B}}=\sqrt5\cdot\sqrt2\approx3.16$,
with equality exactly for the targeted event $\outcomechange{B}\propto(1,-2)$ of the same
anchored size --- aim along the caring direction and the bound is attained. The holding
follows only once the radius is posted:
$\holding{A}{B}=(1)_+/(\sqrt{10}\cdot1)\approx0.32$. Every line above stalls at a
converter until its row appears in the little ledger --- the toy buys its way past each
stall, in the open, which is \ftag{epi:conv} run at the smallest scale that has it.

\begin{definition}[\hdr{c:exch}]\grade{nec}
  \begin{equation}
    \currency{i}{j}:\dimnodewelfareratio{i}{j},
    \qquad
    \currency{i}{j}>0.
    \label{eq:c:exch}
  \end{equation}
  It converts $j$'s own welfare scalar into $i$'s welfare scalar. No no-arbitrage condition
  is imposed.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[\hdr{f:holonomy}]\grade{nec}
  \begin{equation}
    \holonomy{i}{j}{k}:=\currency{i}{j}\currency{j}{k}\currency{k}{i}.
    \label{eq:f:holonomy}
  \end{equation}
  Its units telescope to $\dimratio$. A universal welfare scale exists exactly when
  \begin{equation}
    \currency{i}{j}=c_i/c_j
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    \holonomy{i}{j}{k}=1\ \text{ on every cycle}.
    \label{eq:f:holonomy-coherence}
  \end{equation}
  Complete alignment need not imply currency coherence: $\leanbare\equiv1$ can coexist with
  $\holonomy{i}{j}{k}\neq1$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[\hdr{nrm:incl}]\grade{nec}
\defines{\holdingtyped}
  The extended self folds each other \textbf{by the other's own measure}, weighted by the
  holding and booked in the holder's currency:
  \begin{align}
    \extself{i} &= \privatewelfare{i}
      + \sum_j \foldterm{i}{j}
      && \dimnodewelfare{i} \label{eq:nrm:incl}\\
    \holding{i}{j} &= \holdingdef{i}{j}
      && \dimratio \label{eq:nrm:holding}\\
    \holding{i}{j} &= \holdingcosform{i}{j}
      && \text{where }\lean{i}{j}\text{ is defined} \label{eq:nrm:holding-cos}
  \end{align}
  At felt-zero $\caringcovector{i}{j}=0$, $\lean{i}{j}$ is undefined but
  $\holding{i}{j}=0$. The radius $\belongingradiusof{i}$ at which holding becomes belonging
  is posted, not given.
\end{definition}

Holding says how much of the edge belongs to the self; currency says in whose welfare-unit
the folded content is booked; $\selfweight{j}$ says whose measure supplies the content.
Folding the other by $\selfweight{i}$ instead is annexation \handle{nrm:annex} --- the
cartel run inside care (\ftag{J:hip}).

\subsection{The two zeros}
\label{subsec:ont:zeros}
\secgrade{nec}%
The cosine vanishes two ways, and they are not one configuration \ftag{g:split}:
\begin{align}
  \leanbare = 0 &\iff \caringcovector{i}{j} \perp \feelingcovector{j},\ \caringcovector{i}{j} \neq 0
    &:& \eqtag{f:ambiv}{the \emph{only} cause}\\
  \caringcovector{i}{j} = 0   &\Longrightarrow \leanbare\ \text{undefined}
    &:& \eqtag{f:feltzero}{the apparent stranger}\\
  \comovfield = 0         &\subsetneq \{\,\caringcovector{i}{j} = 0\,\}
    &:& \eqtag{f:sealed}{structural, the seal}
\end{align}
Felt zero \ftag{f:feltzero} is the apparent stranger: $\caringcovector{i}{j}=0$,
$\leanbare$ undefined, yet $\comovfield\neq0$ --- a real, accountable tie $i$ does not
feel. Net zero is \emph{ambivalence} \ftag{f:ambiv}: real co-moving fate whose
components cancel under the weighting --- a friend in health, a rival in standing, a neutral
lean over a positive stake. The off-diagonal the scalar cannot name lives here; alignment
reads $0$ while stake reads large. Only structural zero is a wall.

\begin{econ}[label=e:lemons, title={The lemons wall}, ref=f:feltzero,
  cites={a:lemons,a:signaling,a:screening}, departs=wall,
  stopsat={takes the no-signal wall as built; reads it as a structural zero, not a friction}]
  Motion along $\leanbare$ is information economics, granted on its own terms:
  $\caringcovector{i}{j}=0$ is
  Akerlof's lemons wall \parencite{a:lemons}, the configuration where no quality signal
  crosses, bridged only at the cost Spence's signaling \parencite{a:signaling} and Stiglitz's
  screening \parencite{a:screening} measure --- we take the wall as they built it and read it
  as a structural zero, not a friction. The transposition is part of the departure and not
  an identification: their wall is epistemic, a claim about what quality can be signalled,
  where $\caringcovector{i}{j}=0$ is ontic --- nothing to feel rather than nothing to read.
\end{econ}

\begin{econ}[label=e:witness, title={Structural holes versus closure}, ref=f:eff,
  cites={a:burt,a:coleman}, departs=flatten,
  stopsat={one axis (broker rent) where the field needs two (rent and density)}]
  The witness axis is network economics --- \textcite{a:burt}'s structural holes and broker
  rent against \textcite{a:coleman}'s closure; the contribution sets them orthogonal and
  studies the field.
\end{econ}

\begin{econ}[label=e:capital, title={Reswitching as honest ambivalence}, ref=op:cos,
  cites={a:capital}, departs=scalar,
  stopsat={a scalar aggregate needs a weighting not exogenous to what it measures}]
  The operator-versus-cosine split has its method-ancestor one domain over, in the
  capital-aggregation controversy \parencite{a:capital}, and the parity is exact: Cambridge
  granted that a scalar aggregate needs a weighting not exogenous to what it measures, and
  \ftag{P:nonorm} is that identical refusal typed --- deny it here and the controversy goes
  with it. Ambivalence is its reswitching read honestly, not borrowed loosely: reswitching
  is a \emph{technique} that reverses as the interest rate moves, and the reversal \emph{is}
  a sign-flip of the net contraction $\ip{\caringcovector{i}{j}}{\feelingcovector{j}}$ as the weighting sweeps ---
  the monotone quantity that turns, the off-diagonal the scalar cannot name. The same
  cancellation, found one aggregate over.
\end{econ}

\subsection{Witness density}
\label{subsec:ont:density}
\secgrade{nec}%
\begin{definition}[\hdr{f:Ncount}]\grade{nec}
  \begin{equation}
    \countanch=
    \sum_w(1-\aligndefect{w})\reach_w
    \qquad \dimcount.
    \label{eq:f:Ncount}
  \end{equation}
  Similarly $\countauth\ \dimcount$.
\end{definition}

Both factors are read against posted objects, not supplied by whoever computes the tally.

\begin{definition}[\hdr{f:defector}]\grade{cont-anch}
  The \textbf{counterfactual defector} is the reference profile the subscript names: for the
  observed party, the alignment profile of a party co-present with the act whose alignment
  that party did not author --- the witness one would face if no witness were one's own. It
  is a profile and not a person, and it is a node of the measured field, which is why the
  discount cannot be computed from inside it (\ftag{J:anchor}). The witness's
  \textbf{alignment defect} is the departure of $w$'s profile from that reference,
  \begin{equation}
    \aligndefect{w}\in[0,1],\qquad\dimratio,
    \label{eq:f:defector}
  \end{equation}
  zero for a witness whose alignment the observed party could not author and one for a
  witness authored outright. So $1-\aligndefect{w}$ is the witness's independence weight,
  and \ftag{f:Ncount} is a tally of independence weighted by reach.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[\hdr{f:reach}]\grade{cont-anch}
  The \textbf{reach} $\reach_w$ is the witness's witnessing weight on the act,
  \begin{equation}
    \reach_w\in[0,1],\qquad\dimratio,
    \label{eq:f:reach}
  \end{equation}
  and it is one object under the two readings this section carries. Under \handle{f:local},
  the depth-one co-present sum and enlargement's anchor, $\reach_w$ is the co-presence
  indicator: one for a party present at the act, zero otherwise. Under \handle{f:path}, the
  depth-$k$ bridged path-reliability and harm's anchor, $\reach_w$ is the reliability of the
  best bridged path from the act to $w$ --- hard-zero under concealment, which severs every
  path. A count is computed under one reading and inherits which; the harm half discounts in
  \ftag*{f:path} and the enlargement half in \ftag*{f:local}. Both summands being
  $\dimratio$, the tally's $\dimcount$ is the summation's own count-grade
  (\ftag{gen:ext}).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[\hdr{f:eff}]\grade{nec}
  The second descriptive axis is $\delta$, neighbourhood density of independent witnesses,
  with the anchored form
  \begin{equation}
    \effdensity[i]=\frac{\countanch}{\countpool},\qquad\dimratio,
    \label{eq:f:eff}
  \end{equation}
  Unit check: $\dimcount/\dimcount=\dimratio$. A crowd is not a density until divided by
  its pool.
\end{definition}

\begin{insight}[\hdr{f:pool}]\grade{cont-anch}
  Coherence pools the value; independence supplies the weight.
\end{insight}

Both readings of the reach carry forward, and the independence qualifier each of them owes
is paid at \ftag{J:anchor}.

What the display does not fix is the range of the sum. A party with reach has the standing
to witness; whether they report is a will (\zcref{W:lookout}), and a will is not a number
the count can read off the field. So the tally must say how a party with reach becomes a
counted witness --- and that is a modelling choice, not a theorem. This document
\textbf{chooses}, and posts the price.

\begin{statement}{mod:recruit}{The motivation-gated recruitment reading}
  The index $w$ in \ftag{f:Ncount} runs over the \textbf{recruited} witnesses and not over
  every party with reach. Recruitment is a Bernoulli draw whose probability is the belief
  layer's motivation factor \ftag{bf:mot}, so the tally's $\sum_w$ is an expectation over
  the recruited set: a party with reach enters it in proportion to their motivation, and a
  neighbourhood in which every party with reach has motivation zero contributes no anchored
  count. The gate acts on the \emph{index set} and never as a factor in the display, which is
  what leaves the count motivation-free and the alternative below a genuine reading of the
  same equation rather than a different one. The alternative is a
  \textbf{positional} reading, in which the sum ranges over every party with reach and
  motivation touches only the reader's weighting of a report already made. Its price is
  posted where the bridge is spent: on the positional reading the cartel's deterrent
  collapse is a claim about the credibility of reports and not about the density
  (\see{subsec:ont:opposition}).
\end{statement}

\subsection{Accountability --- the first gate}
\label{subsec:ont:accountability}
\secgrade{cont-anch}%
\begin{definition}[\hdr{f:acc}]\grade{cont-anch}
  Accountability gates on capacity and charges on realization:
  \begin{align}
    &\accountability = \capgate{i}{j}\,(1+\effdensity)
      &:& \eqtag{eq:f:acc}{the gate, $\dimratio$: answerable at all?}\\
    &\realizedstake{i}{j}
      &:& \eqtag{eq:f:charge}{the charge, $\dimwelfare$ (\ftag*{f:stake})}
  \end{align}
  The operator norm compares anchored lengths top and bottom, so the gate reads
  $\dimratio$ with no reference perturbation posted.
\end{definition}

This is the gate-stride \ftag{g:gate} at \zcref{tbl:sc:acc}, and it is one-sided:
$\accountability=0\iff\capgate{i}{j}=0\iff\comovfield=0$. Harm realized by a particular
$\outcomechange{j}$ is priced at $\realizedstake{i}{j}$, which vanishes on
$\ker\comov{i}{j}$ --- a coupling that happens to miss today is still answerable, because
the gate never consulted the act. The amplifier
$1+\effdensity\ge1$ never vanishes --- it scales, it does not gate; the on/off is the capacity
alone. The spot cost is felt by the victim now, costless to the actor; the forward is the
actor's loss, a claim priced in anchored density, maturity $\horizon$ the densification time,
inert until $\effdensity>0$.

Accountability gates on capacity and charges on realized stake --- neither operation uses
alignment --- and each object carries its own reason.
Dimension separates them: the charge is a $\dimwelfare$ where the lean is a $\dimratio$.
Invariance is what makes the wall re-weighting-proof: the capacity $\capgate{i}{j}$
involves neither party's weighting, where a wall built from the direction --- or from
privately weighted felt stake --- would fall whenever either party merely re-weighted.

\begin{corollary}[\hdr{f:seal-law}]\grade{nec}
  The seal is $\comovfield=0$ alone. The three zeros are distinct configurations
  (\zcref{tbl:ont:zeros}): the boundary is dimensional,
  $\partial_{\effdensity}\accountability|_{\comovfield=0}=0$ is the wall,
  $\partial_{\effdensity}\accountability|_{\comovfield\neq0}>0$ everywhere else. Only the genuine stranger is
  sealed; the apparent stranger and the frenemy are held to account. And the wall is
  \textbf{structural, not reachable by neglect}: on the completed branch a neglected
  coupling relaxes to a strictly positive ambient floor wherever a goods space is
  shared (\see{subsec:osc:polar}), so no amount of not-tending authors the seal ---
  only the world's shape does. The genuine stranger has no fate to share; everyone
  else remains answerable, at the strength the surviving channel carries.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{The two rewards}

\secgrade{nec}%

\begin{definition}[\hdr{f:rew}]\grade{nec}
  Against accountability runs the reward field
  \begin{equation}
    \rewardfield \sim \bigl(1-\attenuation(\opnormG{\comovfield})\bigr)\,\rewardpotential(\leanbare)\,(1-\effdensity),
    \qquad \dimratio,
    \label{eq:f:rew}
  \end{equation}
  with $\attenuation(0)=0$, $\attenuation'>0$, and $\rewardpotential\equiv1$ at $\caringcovector{i}{j}=0$ --- an intensive rent-potential

\end{definition}

Distinct from it is $\surplus$, extensive surplus magnitude $\dimwelfare$, realized only by

sit on, surplus $\dimwelfare$ a magnitude you must move to capture. They peak at opposite ends
of $\leanbare$.

\begin{econ}[label=e:rent-surplus, title={Broker rent versus the gains from trade},

  unbridged; $\surplus$ is \textcite{a:smithdiv}'s division of labor and
  \textcite{a:ricardo}'s comparative advantage, value created by bridging. Economics scores
  both as additive contributions to one scalar return; separating them by dimension is what
  exposes that they peak at opposite ends, and that the moral readout \ftag{op:vmoral} was
  subtracting the wrong one.
\end{econ}

\subsection{Gradient opposition, and the sign that sets it}
\label{subsec:ont:opposition}
\secgrade{nec}%
\begin{proposition}[\hdr{f:opp}]\grade{nec}
  Accountability gates on capacity and charges on realized stake, and neither operation uses
  alignment, so $\partial_{\leanbare}\accountability=0$; the opposition lives
  on operator-space. Raising $\opnormG{\comovfield}$ raises $\accountability$ and lowers extractable rent, so
  \ftag{g:sign} gives a global opposition
  \begin{equation}
    \nabla_{\comovfield}\rewardfield\cdot\nabla_{\comovfield}\accountability\le0,
    \label{eq:f:opp}
  \end{equation}
  one move on $\comovfield$, read by its two projections.
\end{proposition}

Hinge constants set the exchange: $\hingeA\ \dimhingeA$ and $\hingeB\ \dimrate$ fix
the rent--surplus rate $\Wstar=\hingeB/\hingeA\ \dimwelfare$ \ftag{c:Wstar}, the open/seal
threshold --- posted, not derived, the framework's first dimensioned exchange-rate.

The sign of $\alignfact$ is defined on the anchored metric $\anchormetric$:
alliance, enmity, and the balanced triad are therefore \gradeword{cont-anch}, two communities
with different $\anchormetric$ disagreeing, both correctly --- the sign-latency dual to the
magnitude-latency at \ftag{dyn:bif}, run on the sign of $\alignfact$ rather than the
existence of $\alignfixed$. The two readings of the stake now split the moral axis. Inclusion folds by $j$'s measure (\ftag{nrm:incl},
\gradeword{cont-comm}); exposure is its sign-blind twin:
\begin{align}
  &\text{exposure} &:& \eqtag{nrm:expo}{$\accountablestake{i}{j}$,
     sign-blind, \gradeword{cont-anch}}
\end{align}

\begin{statement}{nrm:incl-expo}{Inclusion and exposure}
  You \textbf{are} what leans with you (inclusion); you \textbf{answer for} what moves through
  you (exposure).
\end{statement}

They are one magnitude $\dimwelfare$, split by sign \ftag{g:split}. On $\leanbare\ge0$ they
co-vary; they separate at the frenemy --- real stake, net-zero lean --- who is answerable-to
but not part-of. The wedge proves they are two axes.

\refrain{}{Harm is downhill; enlargement is uphill.}

The line holds on $\leanbare\ge0$. At $\leanbare<0$ accountability does not invert ---
$\accountability$ gates on $\accountablestakeof{i}{j}{\outcomechange{j}}\ge0$, sign-blind --- so
common-enemy harm is fully accountable in principle. The inversion lives in two other
channels. The \textbf{reward} rises as a balanced-triad surplus: for $i,j$ allied
($\alignpair{i}{j}>0$) against common enemy $k$ ($\alignpair{i}{k},\alignpair{j}{k}<0$),
\begin{equation}
  \enemysurplus=\underbrace{\alignpair{i}{k}\,\selfweight{k}\transpose\outcomechange{k}}
    _{\text{direct}}+\underbrace{\alignpair{i}{j}\alignpair{j}{k}\,
    \selfweight{k}\transpose\outcomechange{k}}_{\text{via ally}}>0,\qquad\dimnodewelfare{k},
  \label{eq:opp:enemy}
\end{equation}
both terms positive exactly under the cartel sign-pattern --- structural balance --- the
surplus typed in $k$'s unit, $i$'s own booking of it carrying $\currency{i}{k}$ like any
other cross-node read. The
\textbf{deterrent} collapses in parallel, and it collapses across the posted bridge
\ftag{mod:recruit} rather than inside the density's own display, which carries no motivation
term. Inside a cluster uniformly aligned against $k$ \emph{both} factors of \ftag{bf:mot}
fail at once: a witness $w$ drawn from the cluster carries $\alignment{i}{w}>0$, which cuts
the self-protective factor below $\tfrac12$, and $\alignment{w}{k}<0$, which cuts the
sympathy factor below $\tfrac12$, so motivation is under $\tfrac14$ for every one of them
and vanishes in the limit $\alignment{i}{w}\to1$, $\alignment{w}{k}\to-1$. Under the
recruitment reading the cluster therefore supplies no anchored witnesses and
$\effdensity[k]\to0$ on its neighbourhood; under the positional alternative the count is
untouched and what collapses is the credibility of the reports. Either way the density
cannot see the cluster's \emph{coherence} on its own: $\effdensity$ kills copies, and the
clique-confound is colour-span's (\ftag{pr:span}, \ftag{J:anchor}) --- so the collapse is a
recruitment result and not a Sybil one. Surplus up,
density down, accountability intact but unenforced --- the cartel's dark twin, the balanced
triad, exactly where enmity is held in common.

\begin{econ}[label=e:fwt, title={The invisible hand and its blind half}, ref=f:opp,
  cites={a:fwt}, departs=wall,
  stopsat={the premises that prove the interior are the premises that erase the sign}]
  Smith's wager --- self-interest aligned with collective good --- is the conjecture
  $\nabla\rewardfield\parallel\nabla\accountability$; the First Welfare Theorem, within
  Arrow--Debreu \parencite{a:fwt}, makes a version precise under complete markets, no
  externalities, full information. Take the theorem exactly as proved and run it to its edge.
  The same three assumptions that earn the wager on the interior --- complete markets, no
  externalities, full information --- are the assumptions under which $\leanbare<0$ cannot be
  written: a balanced market has no good whose production is another's destruction, no axis
  on which cooperation \emph{toward harm} is the aligned outcome. So the silence is not an
  omission the theorem could repair; it is \emph{entailed} by the premises that make the
  theorem true. You cannot keep the interior and disown the edge --- the complete-markets
  assumption that proves the invisible hand is the same one that erases the sign on which it
  deals the wrong card. Signed $\leanbare$ dates the wager: aligned-against on $\leanbare\ge0$,
  fraying toward the stranger, inverted at $\leanbare<0$, where the wall here makes a
  \emph{derived} feature of what the theorem can only pass over in silence. Grant the FWT and
  you are co-liable for its blind half.
\end{econ}

\begin{econ}[label=e:balance, title={Collusion as the welfare theorems' missing axis},
  ref=f:opp, cites={a:collusion,a:balance}, departs=wall,
  stopsat={welfare apparatus and collusion apparatus are disjoint literatures the field unifies}]
  The dark twin has a native economic literature the framework should not cede to sociology.
  The sign-pattern under which harm to a node is extended-self-rewarded --- $i,j$ allied
  against common enemy $k$ --- is \emph{collusion}: \textcite{a:collusion}'s oligopoly, the
  cartel that cooperates inside precisely to extract outside, tacit-collusion theory's whole
  object. Economics knows the structure intimately, in its \emph{positive} register; what it
  lacks is a \emph{normative} one. Structural balance --- Heider; Cartwright--Harary
  \parencite{a:balance} --- gives the sign-condition, and the welfare theorems have no axis
  on which to read it as the harm it is --- because, by \ftag{e:fwt}, the premises that would
  let them score it are the premises that assume it away. The departure is not that economics
  cannot see the cartel --- oligopoly theory sees nothing else --- but that its welfare
  apparatus and its collusion apparatus are two disjoint literatures the signed field unifies
  on one axis.
\end{econ}

The expansive will \ftag{w:exp} reaching for an unpriced complementarity is
\virtue{wonder}, and what it finds on the far side is \virtue{beauty} --- seeded here,
harvested at the foot. In the seed regime the crossing is a finite turn paid before the
reward exists (\see{sec:fixed-points}).

\begin{theorem}[\hdr{w:torque}]\grade{nec}
  The subscript was never free. On the stochastic branch the lean of a directed edge is
  the cosine of the \emph{tail's} caring direction, and a will is a torque on that
  direction --- a drift on the tail's own sphere; nothing in the branch's ontology lets
  any other agent apply it. \textbf{The forcing term of a directed edge belongs to its
  tail}: every will written on $\alignpair{i}{j}$ is the tail's own spend, and every
  other party reaches the edge only through its parameters. Even the head is a third
  party to your caring: $j$ moves $i$'s lean only by moving the shared surplus --- by
  being lovable, not by turning $i$'s heart.
\end{theorem}
