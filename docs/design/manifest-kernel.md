# The manifest kernel and its serialized boundary · `proposal:manifest:kernel-bite`

## Boundary · `sec:manifest:boundary`

The manifest enters in two separately reviewed bites. M1a admits the Rust-data kernel, its structural validator and a seed together; M1b admits the serialized boundary only with its first consumer, M6's projection generation and checking path. M1a establishes that identities resolve, relations satisfy their declared contracts, status does not overstate what the implementation knows, and coverage cannot claim an audit that has not occurred. Neither bite establishes a mathematical verdict. The authority is Rust data; serialization is an interchange representation of that authority, not another place to author it (`dec:graph:closed-authority`, `dec:graph:status-and-coverage`, `dec:process:signed-bites`).

The admitted vocabulary comprises four node identities, two node kinds, two relation kinds, one unconditional guard form, two authored maturity values, derived identification, calibration and evidence, and an explicit partial coverage declaration. Both relation kinds carry their own endpoint and cycle declarations. A closed vocabulary need not anticipate every eventual member: adding a space, reading, converter, calibration model or conditional guard requires its own checked extension. In particular, this bite does not admit an untyped space placeholder that a later bite must repair.

The glossary defines architectural vocabulary, including claim, referent and coverage, but supplies no definitions of overlap quantities, spaces or mathematical claims (`reg:glossary:terms`). The README names overlap, co-movement and caring without giving their mathematics (`guide:ftoo:overview`). Inventing their endpoint contracts would give the validator invented theory to approve. The seed therefore admits the audit trial's objects pending their prose: the first and third coefficients of the three-dimensional Langevin expansion and their value claims. They are proposed admissions grounded in executable trial content, not a referent expansion of an existing paper passage. No repository prose yet supplies their editorial formulation; all four enter with maturity `proposed`. Their permanent identities do not become reusable merely because their formulations remain provisional.

The seed's claim descriptions are explanatory prose, not an expression language, proof certificate or guard. Claim statements remain the later producers' responsibility (`dec:audit:engine-of-record`). The kernel checks the graph about these statements; it cannot establish the statements by checking their descriptions. All claims remain explicitly deferred even though the standalone trial has checked the corresponding coefficients: that trial is neither a manifest producer registry nor a retained manifest audit report.

M1a changes only the manifest crate's `src/` modules assigned to it below. Its draft API needs no new dependency: `Cargo.toml` and `Cargo.lock` remain unchanged, and Serde, serde_json, a loader, writer, schema version and wire fixtures do not enter M1a. Its validator-with-layer evidence is the graph validator consuming the seed and rejecting mutations through the draft API. It contains no command-line tool, committed projection, notation registry, document change, engine dependency or public audit adapter (`arg:process:reasons`).

M1b adds `src/wire.rs`, wire tests, serialization dependencies in `crates/ftoo_manifest/Cargo.toml` and their `Cargo.lock` entries, together with the projection generation/check library path in `crates/ftoo_codegen`. This JSON is the ADR 006 projection encoding, not a separate audit-input format. Its first consumer is M6's projection check: load the actual generated bytes, reconstruct the validated graph and compare every node and edge against the Rust authority before the shared generation path performs its stale-file byte comparison. The committed artifact is `generated/manifest.json`; write and check use the same canonical writer. This keeps one semantic projection for later audit and runtime consumers and makes the every-edge guarantee testable at admission. M1b cannot land as an unused codec or merely an in-memory round-trip test; it waits for this consumer even if M2 or M3 lands first. M6 may admit this projection slice before its separate unit and document work.

## M1a: modules and types · `sec:manifest:types`

The crate follows the existing audit crate's library-first shape: a documented `lib.rs`, private implementation modules, explicit public re-exports and `#[cfg(test)] mod tests` in `src/tests.rs`. Public fallible APIs document their errors; tests return a result and distinguish the expected refusal from unrelated failures. There is no new binary. Package edition, Rust version, license, publication setting and lints continue to inherit from the workspace. The inspected workspace specifies edition 2024, Rust version 1.99.1 and toolchain `nightly-2026-11-03`; the implementation uses the workspace's choices without revising them. Clippy all and pedantic remain denied, CI supplies `-D warnings`, and all build commands use `--locked` (`dec:workspace:norms`).

|Module|Responsibility|
|---|---|
|`src/lib.rs`|Document the assurance boundary and re-export types and functions.|
|`src/identity.rs`|Closed identifiers, explicit semantic-name mappings, expected kind and the exhaustive identity list.|
|`src/graph.rs`|Draft and validated graph types, payloads, edges, guards, relation contracts and immutable accessors.|
|`src/status.rs`|Authored maturity and the single derivation of the three remaining axes.|
|`src/coverage.rs`|Coverage types and the kernel's partial-release predicate.|
|`src/validation.rs`|Numbered register, staged structural checks, deterministic diagnostics and relation-local traversal.|
|`src/seed.rs`|The four proposed trial objects, their edges and the partial coverage declaration as Rust data.|
|`src/tests.rs`|M1a's positive controls, independently stated expectations, draft mutations and API tests; M1b later adds byte-boundary negatives.|

M1a uses the standard library alone. M1b adds Serde and serde_json as its only new runtime dependencies and locks their resolved public releases in `Cargo.lock`. Neither bite adds the calculation engine to the manifest. M1b implements strict object visitors directly rather than deserializing through a generic map that has already discarded duplicate keys; no dependency-specific flatten behavior is part of the contract.

### Identity and payload · `sec:manifest:identity`

`NodeId` has exactly the four variants below. Each variant has an explicit semantic-name accessor and an expected `NodeKind`. M1a fixes this exhaustive identity table; M1b uses it for parsing and writing, never serializing variant names by convention. Renaming a Rust variant cannot change identity. Labels below specify the future Rust mints; they are neither Markdown mints nor citations to earlier definitions. The title uses the Document proposal kind; section labels identify sections, and register labels identify enumerated inventories, in keeping with the label convention (`conv:adr:labels`).

|Rust variant|Semantic name|Kind|Maturity|Seed description|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|`LangevinCoefficientOne`|`def:langevin:coefficient-one`|`quantity`|`proposed`|The coefficient of k in the three-dimensional Langevin expansion.|
|`LangevinCoefficientThree`|`def:langevin:coefficient-three`|`quantity`|`proposed`|The coefficient of k cubed in the same expansion.|
|`LangevinCoefficientOneValue`|`claim:langevin:coefficient-one-value`|`claim`|`proposed`|The first coefficient is exactly 1/3.|
|`LangevinCoefficientThreeValue`|`claim:langevin:coefficient-three-value`|`claim`|`proposed`|The third coefficient is exactly -1/45.|

Quantity identities name definitions; value identities name mathematical claims, so their `def` and `claim` kinds fit what they admit. The Markdown-only `tools/check_labels.py` cannot see these Rust mints. Cross-surface uniqueness between Rust and Markdown mints remains unchecked through M1a and M1b until M6's label-integration slice joins the exhaustive Rust identity export to harvested Markdown mints, rejects intersections and duplicate mints, and tests an intentional collision. The ordinary labels gate must not be reported as proving this invariant.

The mathematical source is `crates/ftoo_audit/src/lib.rs`, whose recurrence is `(m + 2)c_m = [m = 1] - sum(c_i c_j, i + j = m - 1)`, and `crates/ftoo_audit/src/tests.rs`, whose independently calculated series oracle includes these two values. This source has no minted document label; the file reference is not presented as an ADR citation. The coefficient-three quantity depends on coefficient one through the recurrence, and the coefficient-three value claim uses the coefficient-one value claim as a premise.

`NodeDraft` contains `id`, `maturity` and `payload`. `NodePayload` is a closed sum: `Quantity { description }` or `Claim { description, awaits }`. `description` is nonblank explanatory text; it cannot supply executable mathematics, guard syntax, a type or identity. `Awaiting` initially has only `AuditBinding`, whose proposed M1b spelling is `audit-binding`. A claim cannot omit what it awaits. There is no optional producer name, certificate, evidence URL, calibration node or unvalidated arbitrary metadata bag. `NodeKind` is derived from the payload and has exactly `Quantity` and `Claim`; M1a checks this against the identity's expected kind, and M1b checks the wire discriminator through the same path.

The two quantities and two claims meet the two-member admission criterion (`dec:graph:closed-authority`). They do not authorize arbitrary quantities without space typing: these payloads register referents and descriptions only. Admission of quantities participating in mathematical operations requires M2's typed payload extension. Graph validity alone must never be used as evidence that an operation on these quantities is well typed.

`ManifestDraft` contains `nodes: Vec<NodeDraft>`, `edges: Vec<Edge>` and `coverage: Coverage`. The vector representation retains duplicate identities until validation. `ValidatedManifest` has private fields and can be obtained only by validation; M1b's loader later calls that same validation. It exposes immutable nodes, edges, lookup, status, coverage and relation-specific dependency order. It cannot be mutated through public references, and M1b does not add direct deserialization. The seed entry point returns the result of validating its draft, not an unchecked static graph.

### Relations and guards · `sec:manifest:relations`

`Edge` contains `relation: RelationKind`, `source: NodeId`, `target: NodeId` and `guard: Guard`. Source is the dependent for `depends-on`; target is what it needs. Edges are directed records; a symmetric relation requires both orientations. There is no coarse edge-family cycle switch and no inferred endpoint contract. `RelationKind::contract()` returns a static endpoint-pair table, `acyclic`, `allow-self` and `symmetric`; tests iterate the exhaustive relation list. Only compatibility is symmetric.

|Relation spelling|Permitted source → target kinds|Acyclic|Self edge|Meaning and refused wrong edge|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|`depends-on`|quantity → quantity; claim → quantity; claim → claim|yes|no|An object needs another object; quantity → claim is refused, so an object is not defined by the later claim about its value.|
|`compatible-with`|quantity → quantity|no|no|The two quantity declarations are jointly admissible under their stated assumptions, without choosing alternative assumption routes; claim → quantity is refused, so compatibility cannot stand in for a claim's premise or evidence.|

Compatibility records joint admissibility: a consumer may present the pair together under the declarations' shared assumptions rather than as mutually exclusive alternatives. It does not certify either value, license substitution, prove equality or establish transitive compatibility with a third object. The seed's two coefficients belong to the same dimension-three expansion, so neither requires switching to an alternative assumption route. The validator checks this assertion's structural contract, not its mathematical truth. Both directed edges with the same guard are required by M021; neither validation nor serialization silently inserts the reverse. Deleting either direction is a defect. Their permitted two-cycle is the positive control against accidentally imposing dependency acyclicity on the whole graph. Longer cycles are permitted only with every reverse present; self edges remain forbidden.

`Guard` initially has the single variant `Always`; its proposed M1b object is `{"kind":"always"}`. M1a requires this typed guard on every edge, with no string expression or prose condition alternative. Introducing conditional guards before there are typed operands and a checking consumer would reopen the exact hole the graph decision closes (`arg:graph:closed-authority-reasons`). A later conditional-guard admission must decide its typing, satisfiability obligations and effect on dependency traversal together; the present traversal treats every edge as unconditional.

The validator performs cycle detection separately for each relation declaring `acyclic = true`. It traverses all present nodes of its permitted kinds, not merely the coverage slice; a self cycle is rejected by the self-edge check before cycle analysis. Relation-specific dependency order puts targets before sources and breaks independent ties by semantic name. It is available only for acyclic relations; requesting it for compatibility returns a typed unsupported-operation error. An audit scheduler later combines the relevant premise and route information explicitly rather than treating this helper as a complete audit schedule (`dec:audit:engine-of-record`).

### Status · `sec:manifest:status`

`Status` always presents four axes. Maturity is authored: `proposed` means the referent's formulation is provisional; `retained` means it is retained as an editorial formulation. Neither means mathematically certified. All seed objects use `proposed`, because they enter from the audit trial pending their prose. Identification is derived as `identified` from admission of a closed identity with its expected payload; no anonymous or unresolved referents are admitted in this kernel. Calibration is derived as `not-applicable` because the two admitted payload forms contain no empirical calibration contract. Evidence is derived as `not-a-claim` for quantities and `deferred` for claims, with the claim payload's required `awaits` identifying the blocker.

These singleton identification and calibration vocabularies are intentional closed initial sets, not predictions about the full theory. Later payloads that can be unidentified or calibrated extend the derivation and its tests in the same bite; they do not reinterpret `not-applicable` as “unknown”. Similarly no certified, refuted or undecided evidence value is accepted before M3 provides its consumer. There is no authored evidence enum in `NodeDraft`, no calibration node kind and no second status table (`dec:graph:status-and-coverage`).

M1a exposes the four axes through immutable accessors and has no authored derived-axis assertion to compare. M011 holds structurally; M012 is deferred to M1b. M1b's wire form includes all four axes: the writer computes them, and the loader retains their assertions temporarily, compares them to the derivation and rejects disagreement rather than silently overwriting it. The checked representation discards those redundant assertions.

### Coverage · `sec:manifest:coverage`

`Coverage` contains `slice: Vec<NodeId>`, `closed_world: ClosedWorld` and `release_gate: ReleaseGate`. The proposed M1b wire spellings are `slice`, `closed-world` and `release-gate`. `ClosedWorld` has three required Boolean fields: `referents`, `relations` and `audit`; true would assert respectively that the admitted referents exhaust the intended theory, that the relations exhaust its intended semantic dependencies, and that the audit binding exhausts the declared claim domain. False makes no completeness claim. These are coverage assertions, not permission to leave dangling graph references or unknown kinds; those always fail.

The slice means the claims reached by the manifest-bound audit, not all claims registered or named as future work. Every slice member must be present, unique, a claim and audit-eligible. No claim is eligible in M1a because `AuditBinding` is still its blocker, so the only valid slice is empty. All three closed-world flags must be false: an enumeration of four admitted names does not prove the paper's total universe, and no binding checker exists yet. `ReleaseGate` admits `ExplicitPartial` and `Complete`, but M1a validation refuses `Complete`. M1b's proposed tokens are `explicit-partial` and `complete`; a complete request must reach a coverage diagnostic rather than being treated as a misspelling.

This is an implemented conservative predicate, not an unchecked TODO: only an empty audited slice, false closure flags and an explicit partial gate pass. Registered but deferred claims remain visible through node status and `awaits`. An empty claim set must not make a complete release vacuously valid. M3 replaces this predicate only alongside both-direction producer checking and a precise treatment of deferred, refuted and undecided outcomes; the later verdict report must list deferred claims even when outside the reached slice (`arg:graph:status-coverage-reasons`, `arg:graph:status-coverage-consequences`).

## M1b: serialized boundary and first consumer · `sec:manifest:serialization`

Everything in this section is deferred in M1a. M1b lands jointly with the M6 projection generation/check consumer named in the boundary; the JSON is the ADR 006 projection and retains every edge, including both orientations of compatibility. The form below specifies the kernel vocabulary. If M2 or M3 precedes M1b, their new payloads and evidence predicates must receive explicit wire contracts and consumer tests at M1b's review; the codec must not omit those objects or restore the kernel's earlier coverage restrictions. M1a itself freezes no JSON schema.

The graph artifact is one UTF-8 JSON object with exactly `schema`, `nodes`, `edges` and `coverage`. `schema` is the integer token `1`. Every field is required; unknown fields, explicit nulls, omitted defaults and unknown enum strings fail. Record field names and enum spellings are kebab-case; semantic identities retain their minted colon-separated label spelling. Arrays preserve input multiplicity until the relevant uniqueness check. JSON is chosen for one small graph boundary; statement-unit TOML remains M6's separate boundary (`dec:serialization:strict`, `dec:units:statement-units`).

Each node object has exactly `id`, `kind`, `description` and `status`, plus `awaits` for a claim. A quantity with `awaits` is invalid. Each status object has exactly `maturity`, `identification`, `calibration` and `evidence`. A claim node can therefore be written as follows; whitespace is illustrative rather than the writer's byte format.

```json
{
  "id": "claim:langevin:coefficient-one-value",
  "kind": "claim",
  "description": "The first coefficient is exactly 1/3.",
  "status": {
    "maturity": "proposed",
    "identification": "identified",
    "calibration": "not-applicable",
    "evidence": "deferred"
  },
  "awaits": "audit-binding"
}
```

An edge has exactly `relation`, `source`, `target` and `guard`. Coverage for this seed is `{"slice":[],"closed-world":{"referents":false,"relations":false,"audit":false},"release-gate":"explicit-partial"}`. No numerical quantity value enters a JSON number field in this bite: exact values in the descriptions are prose, not parsed mathematics. The only number field is the schema integer. Floating-point and exponent number tokens are rejected everywhere, including `1.0` and `1e0`; adding numeric content later requires an explicit exact integer or rational representation and its canonicality rules, never a float field. There is no opaque expression string that a consumer may evaluate.

The loader accepts bytes, not a caller-preparsed JSON value. Phase one validates UTF-8 without replacement and refuses a BOM. Phase two parses the whole JSON document, rejecting trailing data, duplicate object keys at every nesting depth and all noninteger number tokens. Key comparison uses decoded strings, so `"id"` and `"\u0069d"` are duplicates. Its intermediate value is a closed tree of objects represented as ordered sequences of decoded key/value pairs, arrays, decoded strings, Booleans, integer-only number lexemes and a null sentinel used solely for phase-three rejection. Integer lexemes preserve arbitrary-length decimal input without rounding or floating conversion; phase three checks their field-specific bounds. Delaying null rejection ensures a later duplicate key is still refused before structural errors. Object key uniqueness is checked before storing each pair; no generic map can discard duplicates. The parser's existing recursion bound is retained and refusal is an input error, not bypassed by raising limits. Phase three checks required and permitted fields, discriminators, closed tokens, scalar types and schema. Phase four invokes M1a's graph validation and compares derived status assertions. A duplicate key nested inside an unknown object therefore fails in phase two before phase three reports the unknown field. An error anywhere in an earlier phase prevents later phases from running.

Hand-written node dispatch handles the flattened common and variant fields. Read the unique keys, determine `kind`, then enforce the exact allowed field set for that variant. Do not combine flatten annotations with a promise of deny-unknown behavior. The same explicit exact-field policy applies recursively to status, coverage, closure flags, guard and edge objects. A typo at any depth is rejected. This is the direct regression closure for the flattening lesson (`reg:notes:negative-knowledge`, `arg:serialization:reasons`).

`load(bytes)` returns either `ValidatedManifest` or a typed load error. M1a's `validate(draft)` returns either `ValidatedManifest` or graph diagnostics and is the same structural path used by loading; wire-only assertions are compared before the loader returns the result. `to_bytes(validated)` is the sole canonical writer and returns bytes through a fallible serialization API. Sort nodes and coverage members by semantic name and edges lexicographically by relation spelling, source name, target name and guard sort token. Lexicographic order means unsigned byte order of the UTF-8 spellings, not locale, Unicode normalization or Rust discriminant order. The guard sort token is its object's `kind` spelling, initially `always`. Object keys appear in the orders specified above, with claim `awaits` last. Emit compact JSON and one terminal LF. Escape quotation marks and backslashes, use the short JSON escapes for backspace, tab, LF, form feed and CR, and encode the other U+0000–U+001F control characters as lowercase four-digit Unicode escapes. Emit all other characters literally in UTF-8: no solidus escaping, ASCII-only conversion or Unicode normalization. Input whitespace, escaping and array ordering need not be canonical, but writing any accepted representation yields canonical bytes.

M001 diagnostics carry a zero-based byte offset: the UTF-8 validator's first invalid position or zero for a BOM. M002 parser, duplicate-key and trailing-data errors and M003 lexical number errors carry serde_json's line and column, preserved as reported rather than advertised as byte offsets; a JSON path is included when available. M003 schema-value errors and M004 typed-shape errors, including nulls, carry a JSON path. Missing fields identify the containing object's path and the missing field name. Later graph errors use semantic IDs and draft positions, and M012 identifies the status axis by JSON path. No offset is fabricated from serde_json's line and column.

M1b's first consumer generates `generated/manifest.json`, loads those actual bytes and compares the complete node and edge sets to the validated source. Its graph comparison must fail if a directed compatibility edge or any dependency edge is dropped; its byte check must fail with a readable diff on stale output. Generation and check share the canonical writer and the declared Rust authority inputs. The loader rejects tampered status assertions before the consumer sees a graph. A committed golden alone does not meet this consumer obligation (`dec:generation:byte-gated`).

## Registered validation rules · `reg:manifest:validation`

Numbers are permanent within this proposed manifest register; they are not claimed to be inherited from an unseen implementation. States below are M1a's states, with the M1b transition stated explicitly. A structural rule is made unrepresentable in admitted Rust data; M1a proves its API boundary with compile-fail documentation tests and exhaustive typed cases, while byte-boundary negatives wait for M1b. An enforced M1a rule has a draft mutation that reaches its runtime check. M001–M004 and M012 are registered but deferred to M1b because no bytes or asserted derived values enter M1a. M016–M020 remain deferred to their named domain consumers in both slices unless those successors have landed independently (`dec:graph:validation-rules`).

|Number|State|Statement and scope|Defect refused; required negative|
|---|---|---|---|
|M001|deferred to M1b; then enforced|For every load, UTF-8 is valid and no BOM occurs.|M1b: malformed UTF-8 or a BOM prepended to the seed.|
|M002|deferred to M1b; then enforced|For every object at every depth, keys are unique after JSON decoding; the input is exactly one JSON document.|M1b: duplicate seed `id`, escaped-equivalent duplicate, nested duplicate and a second trailing document.|
|M003|deferred to M1b; then enforced|At every byte boundary, numeric tokens are integers; the kernel wire schema is exactly integer 1.|M1b: change schema to `1.0`, `1e0`, 2 or an oversized integer; no rounding or coercion.|
|M004|deferred to M1b; then enforced|Every wire object admits exactly its specified fields and scalar types; all are required and variant-specific fields are exclusive.|M1b: misspelled status key, extra quantity `awaits`, missing claim `awaits`, null guard, unknown field inside guard or coverage.|
|M005|structural in M1a and M1b|Every identifier, kind, relation, guard and status value belongs to its closed enum; payload determines node kind.|M1a: compile-fail examples passing a string as an ID or guard and naming an absent variant; exhaustive payload-to-kind tests. |M016|deferred: M2|Shared weighting compares paths of the same kind only, for the holding and covector payloads that actually carry such paths.|Different weightings within one kind must fail; an opposite-end weighting across kinds must not. No weighting paths are representable yet.|

|M019|deferred: M2|Each admitted indexed-space family and operation is checked by a family-level typing rule at its node index.|A cross-index operation must fail without per-symbol exceptions. No operation or space payload is admitted here.|
|M020|deferred: M3|Every bound claim has exactly its producer and every producer has a claim; audit routes, budgets, premise readiness and evidence are checked by their consumers.|A missing or orphan producer, conjunctively combined exclusive routes or unbound verdict must fail. The standalone trial supplies none of this binding evidence.|
|M021|enforced|Every edge of a symmetric relation has the reverse edge with the same relation and guard; currently this applies only to compatibility between quantities.|Delete either compatibility direction from the seed. The remaining direction must fail; dependencies require no reverse.|

M021 has its own number because symmetry is a relation obligation distinct from tuple uniqueness: a unique edge can still lack its reverse. M1a enforces M006–M010, M013–M015 and M021, and makes M005 and M011 structural; M1b adds enforcement of M001–M004 and M012 without duplicating the graph checks. M016–M019 await M2 and M020 awaits M3. All 21 entries are present in M1a's register, including the five rules deferred to M1b.

M1a checks drafts in dependency stages: validate node uniqueness, payload identity and nonblank descriptions; resolve references; check endpoint contracts, duplicate/self edges and slice shape; then check symmetry, relation cycles and coverage eligibility/completeness. M1b runs these after wire admission and also compares status assertions. A failed prerequisite prevents the dependent check for that object, so a missing endpoint never becomes an invented kind or panic. Independent errors within a stage are accumulated. Diagnostics sort by rule number, semantic identity or edge tuple, then location; draft array indices are retained for locations but never decide semantic order. A cycle diagnostic reports a deterministic concrete cycle, starting from the smallest semantic name and exploring successors in name order.

`RuleId`, `RuleState` and `RuleSpec` expose the register as library data. Each `RuleSpec` contains number, statement, scope, state and a deferred reason where appropriate. Each graph diagnostic contains rule ID, relevant semantic IDs, an optional edge tuple, draft location and a human explanation. M1b's loading errors use the rule-specific location forms specified above. No diagnostic relies on “validation failed” alone. No warnings stand in for refusals. An enforced rule is not allowed to be absent from the test inventory, and an implementation must not silently mark a deferred rule enforced because its scope happens to be empty.

## Review questions · `sec:manifest:questions`

There are three contestable boundary choices. Each has a recommended disposition that fully specifies the implementation if accepted; none is left for the implementer to decide silently.

**Seed admission.** Should the first seed instead name overlap or caring quantities? Retain the coefficient seed as proposed audit-trial objects pending their prose because it has actual mathematical content in the reference tree and an independent existing oracle, whereas the glossary gives no definitions of those quantities. The alternative needs a reviewed mathematical definition before implementation, not a guessed graph contract. Permanent identity admission does not establish retained editorial formulation, and the trial's success does not transfer certification into status.

**First status and coverage vocabulary.** Should this bite expose future calibrated, certified and complete states? Retain the minimal derived axes and the enforced partial-only predicate. It is more informative to refuse an unsupported completeness assertion than to provide a rich-looking status enum whose evidence cannot be checked. The cost is an explicit schema evolution with M2 and M3, which strict serialization already requires (`arg:serialization:consequences`).

**Cycle-permitting relation.** Is compatibility useful enough to admit with only the two coefficient objects? Retain it as the symmetric assertion of joint admissibility under the declarations' assumptions, enforced by M021's paired-edge requirement. Consumers may present this pair together without an alternative-route choice; they may not infer certified values, substitution or transitive compatibility. Its wrong-kind refusal and permitted two-cycle establish the relation-policy distinction ADR 001 requires without making compatibility an audit premise.
