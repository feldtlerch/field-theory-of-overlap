# ADR 052: Native virtue occurrences · `rec:adr:052-virtue-occurrences`

This record admits reference-mode occurrences in native prose. Ownership transfer awaits an admitted native owning passage and its documentary-target route; this admission creates no first-use mint.

**Decision (Statement)** · `dec:documents:native-virtue-occurrences`

A virtue occurrence is a reference-bearing word, not an inline style. The paper's first occurrence of a roster word owns its existing v:word destination and is bold upright; later paper occurrences and all explainer occurrences are bold italic references to that owning occurrence. The list form only references and never changes first-use state. The semantic object sem:weld:word, its paper tag v:word, the PDF label key and its auxiliary destination, and the word printed to the reader remain distinct fields. None is admitted to the theory graph by converting prose.

The native insertion @virtue{word} retains a located literal roster word. This admission requires reference mode at every native occurrence; a shipped native first use refuses, and all owning occurrences remain inherited. Ownership transfer awaits a separate later admission of an owning passage and its documentary-target route. The shipped ledger checks actual mode, one first-use row and one auxiliary definition per roster word. A reference owns no mint.

The inherited roster is derived by the existing paper binding producer and transported through its captured-input bridge, not copied into an authored word list. No derived legacy origins or new M043 inventory are introduced. A later native declaration would replace the inherited owner only once its paper-origin route is admitted, retaining strict-unit exclusion and without automatic documentary admission.

Paper output uses virtue in the paper and papervirtue in the explainer, preserving their word and link effects. The paper checks the roster; the explainer currently has an empty roster and accepts any argument at that helper, with unresolved targets caught later by the ledger. The checked native roster adds an actual membership check to native explainer occurrences; inherited companion occurrences retain their existing ledger check. The paired explainer requires its paper cross-reference bridge. This admission supplies PDF fragments in the inherited full-document context; a partial standalone LaTeX reading without that context refuses virtue content. Screen output shows each admitted reference word in bold italic with a link to its owning occurrence or unavailable-content destination in the paper reader document. An owning native word requires a later admission and would be bold upright at its public anchor. If the owner is still inherited, ADR 040's explicit end-index destination is used, not a PDF page and not a later coda occurrence. The index entry is titled with the word, has an empty locator and uses a checked public fragment virtue-word. Both writers omit the locator and its separator, yielding “wonder. Not yet available in this rendering.”; recovery and the destination register retain the empty locator. Raw source labels are not reader text. The same fragment moves to the owner's body when it becomes native. This is a new typed reference form, not a reversal of ADR 037's plain-handle policy for tag runs. The see-reference grammar remains limited to heading labels; a virtue key in that form refuses. Its generated bold-italic composition does not admit arbitrary nested style source.

**Reasons (Why)** · `arg:documents:native-virtue-occurrences-reasons`

The words are welded to objects at definite places in the paper. Replacing their macros with bold text preserves ink but loses ownership and navigation. Existing bindings, destinations, preparation, recovery and the shipped ledger preserve that address without a second state authority.

**Declined (Alternatives)** · `arg:documents:native-virtue-occurrences-declined`

A source-order predictor duplicates ownership declarations without proving execution order: moving arguments, floats and trial typesetting can differ from textual order. An authored owner declaration checked against shipped first-use and auxiliary evidence is sufficient; an unchecked flag is not. Treating every reference as a mint makes the explainer a competing owner. Rendering v:word or sem:weld:word in place of the word changes the text. Reusing a generic style run cannot prove a link or mint, while sending screen readers to the PDF ignores the transitional destination contract. A new permanent virtue registry duplicates the inherited roster. A separate history authenticator or stored rerun bundle proves nothing additional about the occurrence being converted.

**Consequences (Accepted)** · `arg:documents:native-virtue-occurrences-consequences`

The coda and explainer closing paragraphs supply the first reference consumers. Owner transfer waits for an owning passage and its metadata route; the appendix list waits for its table. The existing ledger gains mode/plan correspondence; it already proves destination existence and page agreement.

Before retention, forge and HTML observations must cover bold italic linked words, punctuation and narrow wrapping, including the cross-document link and unavailable destination. The approved font files remain the HTML faces. Ordinary independent review covers the machinery and its real consumers under ADR 017/024/039. M077 admits one bounded token kind for the explainer's papervirtue command, comparing its word and ordered semantic identity with each side's captured paragraph and extracted PDF text. The coda paragraph cannot be selected by the present blank-boundary selector: its word evidence is bound PDF byte identity when observed, otherwise ordinary source/text/page review. The paper virtue decoder form enters with the owning-passage consumer, not ahead of it. It proves no face, link or first-use state: the ledger and screen recovery supply their own correspondence, and ordinary review judges appearance. Mechanical content retention still requires the complete conjunction and admission of these complementary proofs; the decoder extension alone authorizes no retention. PDF byte equality is useful evidence when observed, not promised. Reflow alone is not a content defect. Beyond the quoted M077 amendment, no checking scope or accepted self-description is altered by these two prose consumers.
