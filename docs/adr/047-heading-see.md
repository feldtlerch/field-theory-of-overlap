# ADR 047: Paper section and subsection destinations · `rec:adr:047-heading-see`

**Decision (Statement)** · `dec:documents:heading-see`

A located see-reference may resolve a declared paper section or subsection as well as an appendix heading. The shared plan retains the source label, heading kind, source-derived ordinal, owning document and public fragment. The label identifies the heading; its spelling does not determine its kind or number. A bounded reading of the paper's master inclusion order and heading counter events derives the ordinal. Screen locators are §N for a section and §N.M for a subsection, with no separator after §, matching the master's section-type names and empty separator (documents/paper/main.tex:106–107); the pair build confirms the printed result. A decimal component is zero or a nonzero digit followed by digits; leading zeros are refused. The section form is admitted with the subsection form because the parent section event and ordinal already have a checked source consumer and the same bounded locator path has validators for both. The source label must belong to that heading, not merely occur in its file. The paper's typesetter remains independently responsible for the printed counter. Each paper pair compares the planned ordinal with the same label's typeset counter and checks its auxiliary destination against the PDF destination, extending the appendix comparison of ADR 040 (`rec:adr:040-placeholder-index`).

An unavailable heading has one destination in its owning document's end index, with its title, public fragment, locator and explicit unavailability message. Its content is not represented as available. The existing title-derived address and checked override mechanism apply; source labels do not become reader-visible handles. Duplicate destinations, unsupported outline events, absent or ambiguous heading ownership, missing source inputs and counter or destination disagreement refuse at the reference or its evidence site. Availability, index ordering, release refusal and eventual movement of the same destination to its converted body remain governed by ADR 040.

This admission extends resolution, not the inline grammar or the HTML emitter. LaTeX retains the paper's see command, Markdown emits and recovers the planned link, and a paragraph without an admitted HTML emitter retains the registered HTML gap required by M055 and M065. It does not admit starred references, reference lists, explainer-specific reference commands, arbitrary TeX headings or mathematical heading titles.

**Reasons (Why)** · `arg:documents:heading-see-reasons`

The paper already refers to regular headings. In particular, the paragraph in part B refers to “The three tenses”, a subsection in the coda (documents/paper/parts/partB.tex:1051–1053; documents/paper/parts/coda.tex:8–9). The reference has an existing label and typeset destination. Resolving it requires neither a new theory object nor a permanent tag admission.

The target file alone cannot determine its section number. The master starts the section counter at −1 and orders the included parts; a subsection reset also occurs within part E (documents/paper/main.tex:126,133–146; documents/paper/parts/partE.tex:9). Deriving numbers from that source and comparing them with independently typeset values avoids a second authored number list. The source list declares allowable inputs; it does not replace the master's reading order. The bounded profile recognizes the real layout prefix before a heading and the counter-neutral part, gateway, coda and declared-input forms; unsupported embedded heading commands refuse rather than disappear from the count. The master heading scan begins at `\begin{document}`; before that line it checks the named inputs, the section counter initialization and the two exact bibliography penalty settings, and refuses the listed unsupported outline forms, so title-format commands in the preamble are not headings.

The destination resolver and ledger own this extension. Their appendix path also resolves sections and subsections, retaining one destination mechanism and one paper-counter comparison (crates/ftoo_codegen/src/documents/destinations.rs; tools/paper/ledger.py).

The reader refuses counter commands in scanned part and master lines except the fixed section initialization and part E subsection reset; the master's two fixed bibliography penalty lines are numbering-neutral preamble settings. It also refuses literal `\counterwithin`, `\counterwithout`, `\@addtoreset`, `\@removefromreset` and `\numberwithin` forms, redefinitions of `\thesection` or `\thesubsection` through the listed command spellings, `\iffalse`, `\iftrue` and `comment` environment markers in scanned lines, and an appendix switch in the master preamble or body. Each part's headings are counted only within its document environment; the refused forms also refuse in its preamble. Lines after a part's `\end{document}` are not read because the typesetter never sets them. Other conditional forms, alternate redefinition spellings, counter changes made inside macros (including macros in the admitted preamble inputs, which the reader does not read), primitive register assignments and macro-generated or aliased comment behavior are not detected, so the bounded reader does not claim correct numbering for them. An appendix heading with markup that is not the requested target is counted without decoding its title; the former blanket title refusal for that unrequested heading is intentionally removed.

**Declined (Alternatives)** · `arg:documents:heading-see-declined`

Authored screen numbers and inference from a label's prefix are declined: both can disagree with the heading that actually prints. Reading the typesetter's number back as the plan's only number is declined: it would eliminate the independent comparison and make document generation depend on a preceding paper build.

A second outline catalogue, a separate heading registry and a new reference syntax are declined. The master and declared source files already contain the needed order, labels and counter events. A bounded source reader refuses unsupported constructions instead of claiming to interpret general TeX.

Changing an existing native paragraph's reference merely to exercise the extension is declined: it changes what that paragraph points to. A corpus-backed validator can accompany the resolution layer under ADR 009 (`dec:process:signed-bites`), whose decision permits a layer to land with its validator or consumer. The subsequent content conversion remains a separate change.

**Consequences (Accepted)** · `arg:documents:heading-see-consequences`

The source reader yields to ADR 025's complete owning reading inventory under `dec:reading:identified-spines`: M055's fragment target admits only non-heading positions, and composition refuses an undeclared marker, so a generated fragment cannot presently represent a heading in this reader. A change that first represents a heading as a generated fragment adds its checked event before the source reader is removed at authority transfer, with event identity, order and pair-counter correspondence checked across the switch.

Source outline changes can affect references in other files. All outline inputs used to resolve a reference are captured by document preparation and checked for change before its artifacts are retained. The bounded source profile has a maintenance cost: an unsupported counter or heading construction requires an explicit admission with a consumer, rather than a guessed number.

Validation uses the real part B paragraph and the real coda subsection through the unit scanner, label integration, plan resolution, and Markdown emission and recovery of the link. Rust tests read the real master and body sources; Python tests use synthetic counter and destination evidence. The ordinary pair checks the emitted appendix reference. A regular heading enters that retained typesetter comparison when an admitted paragraph emits its reference into a checked document. The part B paragraph remains authored in the paper source, so this admission does not claim a native regular-heading occurrence.

A later content-only conversion supplies insertion acceptance's first eligible candidate. It still needs its own production provenance, source correspondence, screen checks and the evidence admitted by record 045; this record does not make a PDF-byte comparison succeed or widen the mechanical retention rule. HTML reference emission remains separate work, and its gap continues to block a complete release.
