## The Whole Idea, in Plain Words

This first section says everything the theory says, once, in ordinary words. There are no symbols in it and no cross&#45;references; the small&#45;caps marker at the foot of a box or a table is a bookmark for later readers, never vocabulary. Every idea here returns later with its formal name attached. If you read nothing else, read this.

**Ties.** Start with two lives. If something happens to one and nothing at all happens to the other, there is no tie between them. If your bad harvest becomes partly my hard winter — if your fate moves mine — there is. That is the theory’s whole starting point: a relationship is a channel through which fate travels, whatever either party feels about it. Feelings matter, but they are a reading of the channel, not the channel itself.

Every tie invites two different questions. *Which way does it lean?* — when things go well for you, is that good news or bad news for me? And *how much rides on it?* — how much of my fate actually moves when yours does? These are not the same question. A holiday friendship and a marriage can lean the same way; they do not carry the same weight. A rivalry can carry enormous weight and lean against. And two further splits ride under those. A tie has a standing lean, and each act has a lean of its own; a well&#45;leaning friend can land an ill&#45;leaning act — the promotion that genuinely delights him and quietly drowns you out. And “how much rides on it” is itself two questions: what *could* move through the tie, and what an act actually *did* move. You are answerable wherever something could move; you are charged for what did. Keeping direction and amount apart sounds like a small habit; most of what this theory corrects in common sense follows from refusing, ever, to blur them.

**Work.** Left alone, ties loosen. When a tie stays strong for years, that is not stillness; someone is spending effort against the loosening, continuously, the way a hand holds a glass steady on a tilting table. And here is the fine and strange part: a photograph of the glass cannot tell you whether it is simply sitting there or being held against a push. But a *film* can: a held glass trembles — maintenance leaves a signature wobble that mere sitting does not have, and a patient camera can read it. So the faithful work is not invisible after all; watch long enough and you can see *that* a tie is being held. What no camera, however patient, will ever show is what the holding *costs the hand* — by what private weighing of goods the holder counts this tie worth its effort. The record can be given better and better instruments, and the theory welcomes every one; each removes something from the dark. What remains at the end is not a smaller version of the same shadow but a different kind of darkness: not the work, which trembles visibly, but the wage — the price the holder pays in a currency no one else can read. Any honest accounting of people must admit a blind spot in that exact place — and must refuse to read “no visible price” as “no price.”

**Help and cost.** Any act can be sorted by two questions: what did it do to the other person, and what did it do to your own life? Each answer can be *helped*, *untouched*, or *harmed* — nine kinds of act in all, laid out in the first table below. The four cells where no one is harmed and nothing is drained gather in the top&#45;left: the *bright square*, where nothing needs an excuse. The five remaining cells — the whole harmed column plus the whole drained row — run along the right and bottom edges: a *backwards L*. The second table shows the shape bare. The L is where excuses live. One of the theory’s sharpest results, said plainly: the lies people tell themselves operate only on the L. Honest good is often foggy — you rarely know just how good you are — but the fog over the bright square hides nothing. Active self&#45;deception, as this theory defines it, is the repainting of an L&#45;cell as a bright one — the definition draws the boundary, and the result is that the boundary can be drawn at all.

## Accountability

Accountability is about the channel and the charge, never the lean. If your action moves someone’s fate, you answer for the movement — even if you feel nothing, even if your feelings cancel, even if your private weighting hides the tie. The careless landlord who “feels nothing” for a tenant still holds the tenant’s winter in his hands; the not&#45;feeling changes nothing about the holding. And the structure is cleanly two&#45;stage: the *gate* is capacity — could anything move through this edge at all? — and it shuts only for the genuine stranger, where the channel itself is zero; the *charge* is what this act actually moved. An act that happens to miss today, over a live channel, leaves you answerable still: the gate never consulted the act.

## Tests the Theory Can Fail

The theory stakes its empirical standing on a ladder — three instruments, ordered cheapest first, all reading the same class of thing: the part of a tie’s dynamics that *turns* instead of merely growing or fading, which no story of “everyone just follows the payoff gradient” can produce. If relationships were only motion on a landscape of rewards, nothing would ever rotate. Rotation is therefore the theory’s chosen tripwire.

The cheapest rung needs no experiment at all, only a patient record of a *held* tie: if the dynamics have a turning part, the tremor of maintenance does not just jitter — it circulates, and two logged coordinates suffice to catch the circulation in the correlations. &#40;One logged coordinate can never catch it; rotation needs a plane. The instruction “log two things, not one” is itself a falsifiable design principle.&#41; The second rung is release: let a tie go, and if there is a turning part, it does not fade monotonically — it spirals, souring and rekindling at shrinking amplitude, with a fixed pitch. And the two rungs must *agree*: the circulation of the held tremor and the pitch of the released spiral read the same underlying turn, so their disagreement kills the model outright, not a parameter.

## The Signed Externality Log

What, then, can we build? One artifact: a **signed externality log**. Record acts, both valences — harm and help. Price their charge — what actually moved in other lives, read on a rod the log itself publishes: the log’s own measuring axis is posted per entry, criticizable, its lean on the record where every other lean is. Gate on capacity. Log alignment only as context, never as the basis of a charge. Anchor belief in independence and colour&#45;span. Build no global verdict — none crosses the anchor wall.
