Font-dependent defects that recur in every case using them:
(F1) The underbrace character U+23DF (⏟) has no glyph in any installed font. Wherever \underbrace is typeset, it is drawn as a small missing-glyph box (about 8x12 px) under the base, not stretched. The base, the "pending" label and the rest are present. Every \underbrace result below is therefore at best TW in this environment.
(F2) Single Greek letters in math italic (auto-italic MathML mi) are drawn as missing-glyph boxes (α and β in M39).
(F3) mathvariant values other than normal (bold, script, double-struck, sans-serif) are ignored. Those letters draw as ordinary italic.
(F4) mlabeledtr (\tag) and menclose (\boxed) are not laid out as intended (see M28, M34, M46).

SUMMARY (bounded by commit 3e95972, the forge's file view, signed out, dark theme, embedded Chromium 156 on Linux with the observing system's uninventoried installed fonts, 2026-11-18)

1. What rescues the failing equation (M01)
- Dollar-backtick $`...`$ (M02): typesets with the full TeX intact.
- The ```math fence (M03) and the $$ block (M04): typeset.
- Escaping the underscores as \_ inside dollars (M05): typesets, because Markdown strips the backslashes and the engine still gets the subscripts.
- Extra brace groups (M06) do NOT help.
- \( \) (M08) does NOT work: it is not a math delimiter here, and its backslashes are stripped.
- Every typeset form of this equation shares the missing underbrace glyph (F1) in this environment.

2. Characters next to prose that break plain $...$ inline mathematics, and whether $`...`$ avoids each
- Two or more underscores in a paragraph form a Markdown emphasis pair. This happened inside one formula (M01, M06, M09) and across two formulas on one line (M16). B avoids it. A single underscore on a line was harmless (M07, M17, M18, M21, M37).
- Two stars: emphasis (M10). B avoids it.
- "<letters>" such as <y>: removed as an HTML tag (M12, typeset but wrong). B avoids it.
- Backslash before punctuation (\, \; \!): the backslash is stripped by Markdown escaping, so spacing commands turn into literal punctuation (M42, M47). B avoids it.
- Backslash + parenthesis: \( \) lose their backslashes (M08).
- Survived in both forms: \\ (M11), | (M13), ~ (M14), ^ (M15), \frac (M38), Unicode α β ⋅ − (M39 to M41; the Greek glyphs are missing only for font reasons), and a leading or trailing formula on a line (M17, M18).
- No character tested broke the dollar-backtick form in an ordinary paragraph, list item, table cell or heading.

3. Contexts
- Plain-text table cell, bullet list item, h4 heading, and a line with HTML comments next to the formula:
  - $`...`$ works in all of them (M19 to M21, M24).
  - Plain $...$ works only when the formula contains no emphasis-forming pair (the headings with a single underscore work; the underbrace formula fails in the table, the list and next to comments).
  - Table and list structure stayed intact in every case.
- Centred HTML paragraph <p align="center">...</p> (M22):
  - Plain $...$ WORKS (no Markdown emphasis is applied inside an HTML block).
  - $`...`$ FAILS: the backticks reach the engine and print as ‘ ... ‘ around the formula.
- Centred HTML paragraph with <em> inside (M23): NEITHER form is converted to mathematics; both show as italic source. This is the one context where no observed inline wrapper survives.
- Comments right next to a formula (M24) did not stop the B form; A failed only because of the underscores.

4. Fenced ```math versus $$ display
- Both produce display mathematics for every construct.
- Difference: inside ```math, every TeX line ending "\\" reached the engine with an extra backslash ("\\\" + newline), which inserts a control space at the start of the next row (M25 to M27). It is visible as a slight indent in the cases and matrix rows.
- The $$ form passed "\\" exactly and the rows align correctly (M31 to M33).
- In $$ form the display sits inside a <p>; in fence form it is a block of its own. They look the same.

5. Display constructs (same in both display forms)
- aligned, cases and pmatrix: work (with the fence row-start space noted above).
- \tag{7}: broken. The label and equation cells are stacked vertically, there is no right-hand tag, and the equation is unreadable (M28, M34).
- equation environment: renders "x = 1" with NO number, in both forms (M29, M35).
- Long line: no wrapping. It scrolls horizontally inside the formula below about 1012 px of content width; nothing is lost (M30, M36).
- \sum and \int limits: work (M45).
- \boxed: no box drawn (M46).
- \mathbf, \mathcal, \mathbb, \mathsf: present in the MathML but drawn as plain italic (M43).
- \operatorname: refused with a visible page error, and the whole display is not typeset (M44).

6. How failures look
- Markdown failures: raw TeX source in the paragraph, with dollars and backslashes visible. Underscores and stars vanish, and the text between them turns italic. HTML-like <...> disappears.
- Engine-level unknown macros (M48 to M50): no error box. The macro name is printed in red as text and the rest is typeset; arguments become italic letters (amsmath).
- Disallowed macros (M44): a red GitHub flash box "The following macros are not allowed: operatorname" followed by the source in a code block.
- A failure does not affect later formulas (M51).

7. Surprises
- The underbrace, the paper's own construct, never looks right here: its glyph U+23DF is missing (F1), and \boxed draws no box.
- Greek letters in math italic are missing glyphs (F2), and mathvariant styles are ignored (F3).
- These three are properties of native MathML rendering with the observing system's fonts, not of the forge's Markdown parsing. They may look different on a machine or browser with a math font. They are recorded as observed, not generalised.
- Escaped underscores (\_) inside $...$ work as subscripts (M05).
- Plain $...$ inside an HTML block works while $`...`$ there does not (M22).
- Rendering is lazy and happens only when the tab is visible.
- The fenced form adds a backslash at "\\" line ends (M25 to M27).
- No automatic equation numbers exist.
